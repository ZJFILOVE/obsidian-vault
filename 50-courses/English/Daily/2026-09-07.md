# English Input — 2026-09-07

## Today's material
**Adam Savage — How simple ideas lead to scientific discoveries**  
Source type: TED-Ed video with transcript  
Estimated study time: 10–15 minutes  
Source: https://www.ted.com/talks/adam_savage_how_simple_ideas_lead_to_scientific_discoveries?view=transcript

## Why this one
This short talk uses two historical experiments—Eratosthenes' estimate of Earth's circumference and Fizeau's measurement of the speed of light—to show how surprisingly simple experimental ideas can answer very large scientific questions. It is especially useful for practicing the language of measurement, inference, experimental design, and scientific reasoning without requiring specialist vocabulary.

## How to use it
1. **First pass — listen without the transcript.** Write down the two scientific quantities being measured and, in one sentence each, the basic idea behind the measurement.
2. **Second pass — use the transcript.** Notice how the speaker explains a chain of reasoning: observation → assumption → measurement → calculation → conclusion. Mark 4–5 expressions that could be reused in a lab presentation.
3. **Output.** Explain one of the two experiments aloud in English in 60–90 seconds without looking at the transcript.

## Comprehension & output
1. Why was Eratosthenes able to estimate Earth's circumference without traveling around the Earth?
2. What made Fizeau's experiment conceptually simple but experimentally demanding?
3. Think of one experiment from chemistry, biology, or physics in which an indirect measurement reveals a quantity that cannot easily be measured directly. Explain the logic in English.

## Useful lens
While watching, pay attention to the distinction between **measuring something directly** and **inferring it from observable evidence**. This distinction appears constantly in scientific papers and presentations.
