**Amy Adkins — Why do we dream?**

- Type: TED-Ed video + transcript

- Estimated source time: about 5–6 minutes
- Study time: 10–15 minutes

## Why this is worth using

This short lesson presents several competing explanations for dreaming rather than pretending that science has one settled answer. That makes it useful for practicing the language of hypotheses, evidence, uncertainty, memory, and scientific explanation. The narration is clear enough for CET-6 listening while the density of ideas provides a useful bridge toward TOEFL-style academic English.

## Study routine

### First pass — gist, no transcript
Watch once without reading. Do not stop for unfamiliar words. Afterward, write one English sentence answering: **Why is it difficult to give a single explanation for dreaming?**

### Second pass — structure and language
Open the transcript and watch/read again. Identify at least three different theories and note the verb used to express each claim, such as *propose*, *suggest*, *serve*, or *help*. Then choose two expressions you could reuse when discussing uncertain scientific evidence.

### Optional 2-minute output
Explain one theory aloud without looking at the transcript, then add one sentence explaining why it is still only a theory.

## Comprehension and output

1. What does the existence of several theories tell us about the current scientific understanding of dreams?
2. Choose two proposed functions of dreaming. How are their underlying assumptions different?
3. Which explanation seems most plausible to you? Give one reason, while clearly distinguishing your opinion from established evidence.
