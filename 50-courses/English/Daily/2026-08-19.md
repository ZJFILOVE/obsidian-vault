# English Practice — Day 5

## Reading
For many university students, being busy has become almost a sign of success. Students may fill their schedules with classes, internships, clubs and other activities because they believe that using every available hour will help them achieve more. Yet a full schedule does not necessarily mean that time is being used effectively.

Switching between tasks has a cost. Returning attention to the original task requires additional mental effort. Multitasking can therefore create an illusion of productivity: people may feel busy while actually completing important tasks more slowly.

Effective time management is not simply about fitting more activities into a schedule; it is also about protecting one's attention.

### Questions
1. Main idea: **being busy is not necessarily being productive**.
2. Task switching reduces efficiency because **attention must be restored**.
3. `illusion of productivity` = feeling productive while being less efficient.
4. Good time management includes **managing attention**.
