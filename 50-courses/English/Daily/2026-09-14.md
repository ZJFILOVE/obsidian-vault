## Today's material

**HISTORY — 6 Ways Coffee Helped Transform the World**

- **Source type:** feature article
- **Direct source:** https://www.history.com/articles/coffee-history-facts
- **Estimated source time:** about 10–12 minutes
- **Study time:** about 15 minutes for one focused pass, or 20 minutes if you complete the output task

## Why this is specifically worth using

This article starts from an ordinary object—coffee—and uses it to move across several kinds of historical explanation: trade, colonial agriculture, public debate, industrial work, war, consumer culture, and globalization. That makes it useful English practice because you have to follow **causal language across changing contexts**, rather than simply learn a set of facts about one narrow event.

The article is also a good bridge from CET-6 reading toward introductory TOEFL-style nonfiction. Most sentences are accessible, but the text repeatedly uses compact academic and journalistic patterns such as **be tied to**, **fuel change**, **spread across**, **become a commodity**, **enable**, **intervene**, and **be driven by**. These are reusable far beyond history.

### Important caveat about the title

The headline says coffee “helped transform the world.” Read that as a framing device, not as the claim that coffee single-handedly caused the historical changes described. In several sections, coffee is better understood as a **commodity embedded in larger systems**—colonial labor, industrialization, political conflict, military logistics, and consumer culture. One of today's reading goals is to distinguish a vivid causal headline from the more qualified claims actually supported in the article.

## Study routine

### First pass — structure before vocabulary

Read the article once without stopping to look up every word. Your only job is to reconstruct the six-part structure.

After reading, close the page and write six very short notes in English. Each note should answer:

> **What changed, and what role did coffee play in that change?**

Try to use different verbs instead of repeating *changed* or *affected*. Useful possibilities include **enabled, accelerated, reinforced, supported, became associated with, was tied to**.

### Second pass — track the causal language

Read again and mark 5–8 phrases that connect an object, institution, or historical condition to a larger consequence. Do not collect isolated nouns unless they are essential.

Pay attention to four distinctions:

1. **cause vs. contribution** — Did coffee cause something, or did it help make an existing process easier?
2. **product vs. system** — Is the article describing coffee itself, or the labor/trade institutions around it?
3. **short-term use vs. long-term social effect** — For example, a stimulant can help an individual stay awake, while industrial work schedules involve a much larger social system.
4. **fact vs. interpretation** — Which statements are concrete historical events, and which are broader explanations of their significance?

### Output task — 90-second retelling

Without looking at the article, choose **two** of the six sections and explain how they are connected by one larger theme. Possible themes:

- globalization;
- labor and productivity;
- public space and debate;
- war and logistics;
- consumer habits;
- the relationship between everyday products and institutions.

Use this skeleton if helpful:

> At first glance, coffee seems to be just a consumer product. However, in the article it also appears as ________. One example is ________. Another is ________. These cases suggest that an everyday commodity can become important when it is connected to ________.

## Comprehension and output

1. **Why is it more accurate to say that coffee was embedded in systems of trade and labor than to say that coffee alone “caused” globalization or industrialization?**
2. **How did coffeehouses function as more than places to buy a drink? Give one institutional or social role they played.**
3. **Choose one section of the article and rewrite its main claim in more cautious academic English, using a phrase such as _contributed to_, _was associated with_, _helped enable_, or _was tied to_.**

## Optional precision challenge

Rewrite the sentence below so that it makes a weaker and more defensible causal claim:

> Coffee transformed industrial society by making people work longer hours.

Aim for a sentence that separates the **stimulant effect of caffeine** from the much broader organization of industrial labor.
