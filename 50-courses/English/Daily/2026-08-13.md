# English Practice — Day 1

## Reading
University students often believe that studying for longer hours will automatically lead to better academic performance. However, research on learning suggests that the quality of study may be more important than the total amount of time spent studying.

One reason is that human attention is limited. After concentrating on a difficult task for a long period, people often continue working even though their efficiency has already declined. Taking short breaks can help restore attention and make later study more productive.

Another important factor is how information is reviewed. Simply reading the same material repeatedly may create a feeling of familiarity, but familiarity does not necessarily mean that the information can be recalled when needed. Testing yourself, by contrast, forces the brain to retrieve information. This process can strengthen memory and reveal what has not yet been mastered.

Therefore, effective learning is not simply a matter of working harder. Students may benefit more from studying strategically—by managing their attention, testing themselves regularly, and focusing additional effort on their weaknesses.

### Questions
1. Main idea? **Effective study depends on strategy as well as effort.**
2. Why can long study become inefficient? **Attention cannot remain equally effective indefinitely.**
3. Why can repeated reading mislead? **Familiarity can be mistaken for mastery.**
4. `retrieve` most nearly means **bring information back from memory**.
