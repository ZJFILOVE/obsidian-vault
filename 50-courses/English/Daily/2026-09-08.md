## Why this is worth using
This short lesson explains why some experiences become durable memories while others fade. It is useful English practice because the narration is clear but still introduces reusable academic vocabulary such as *encode*, *consolidate*, *retrieve*, *association*, and *stimulus*. The argument also follows a clean explanatory structure that is worth imitating in academic speaking and writing.

## Study routine
### First pass — gist, no transcript
Watch once without pausing. Write a two-sentence English summary answering: **What determines whether a memory becomes strong or weak?** Do not worry about individual unfamiliar words.

### Second pass — language and structure
Watch again with the transcript. Notice how the speaker moves from an everyday example to a scientific explanation. Record 5 useful expressions, then explain one mechanism from the video in your own English without looking at the transcript.

### Optional 2-minute output
Choose one personal memory and explain why it may have become vivid using one idea from the lesson.

## Comprehension & output
1. Why are emotionally significant or frequently recalled experiences often easier to remember than ordinary details?
2. What is the difference between **forming**, **storing**, and **retrieving** a memory?
3. If you wanted to remember material from a university lecture for several months, what study habits would you change based on the lesson? Answer in 3–5 English sentences.
