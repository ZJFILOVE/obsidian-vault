# English Practice — Day 2

## Reading
Smartphones have made information easier to access than ever before. For university students, this can be extremely useful. A difficult term in a textbook can be looked up within seconds, and educational videos can provide explanations that may be easier to understand than written materials.

However, easy access to information may also change the way people learn. When students know that a piece of information can be found online at any time, they may be less likely to remember the information itself. Instead, they may remember where to find it.

This does not necessarily mean that smartphones make people less intelligent. In many situations, knowing how to locate reliable information is an important skill. The problem arises when easy access replaces understanding. A student who can quickly find a scientific formula, for example, may still be unable to decide when or why that formula should be used.

Therefore, technology is most valuable when it supports rather than replaces thinking.

### Questions
1. Main idea: **benefits and potential problems of easy access to information**.
2. Students may remember **where information can be found** instead of the information itself.
3. Access is insufficient if students cannot **understand or apply** information.
4. Author's view: technology should **support independent thinking**.
