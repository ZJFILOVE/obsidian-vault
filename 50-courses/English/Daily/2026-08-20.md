# English Practice — Day 6

## Reading
University students are frequently advised to set clear goals. Goals can provide direction and make it easier to decide which tasks deserve attention. However, setting a goal does not by itself guarantee progress.

People sometimes focus too heavily on the final result. Goals such as “become fluent in English” may be motivating but give little information about what should actually be done today.

Some researchers emphasize systems rather than outcomes alone. A system consists of repeated actions that can be performed regularly: reading for twenty minutes each day, reviewing vocabulary at intervals, or practicing writing several times a week.

Goals provide direction; systems translate that direction into specific behavior. In the long run, progress often depends less on occasional bursts of motivation than on actions that can be repeated consistently.

### Questions
1. Main idea: **goals provide direction, systems turn them into progress**.
2. Distant goals can discourage because they may not specify **immediate actions**.
3. A system = **regularly repeated actions**.
4. Inference: sustainable habits may be more reliable than occasional motivation.
