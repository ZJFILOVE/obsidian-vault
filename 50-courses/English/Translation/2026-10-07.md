## Exercise 1 — CET-6 level

### Topic: campus parcel lockers

Translate the passage before opening the answer.

**某大学在宿舍区增加了一批快递柜，希望减少学生排队取件的时间。最初，学校认为只要增加柜格数量就能解决拥堵，但运行一个月后发现，问题更多出现在特定时间段。下午五点到七点，大量快递集中送达，而不少学生要到晚上才来取件，导致空柜格迅速减少。大件包裹无法放入普通柜格，也会占用人工取件窗口。学校随后开始记录不同时间段的柜格占用率、包裹平均停留时间和超大件比例，并与快递公司协调错峰投递。调整后，晚高峰排队有所缩短，但管理人员认为，一个月的数据还不足以说明新方案在考试周或节假日前同样有效。评价这类服务时，不能只看设备总量，还要看容量是否在真正需要的时候可用。**

Try to use:

- **increase capacity**
- **during peak hours**
- **occupancy rate**
- **average dwell time**
- **stagger deliveries**
- **not sufficient to establish that**
- **available when demand is highest**

> [!answer]- Reference translation
> A university added a new group of parcel lockers near its dormitories in the hope of reducing the time students spent waiting to collect deliveries. At first, the university assumed that congestion could be solved simply by increasing the number of locker compartments. After a month of operation, however, it became clear that the problem was concentrated in particular time periods. Between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m., large numbers of parcels arrived within a short window, while many students did not collect them until later in the evening, causing the number of empty compartments to fall rapidly. Oversized parcels could not fit into standard lockers and also added pressure to staffed pickup counters. The university therefore began tracking occupancy rates at different times of day, average parcel dwell time, and the proportion of oversized items, while coordinating with delivery companies to stagger deliveries. Queues during the evening peak became shorter after the adjustment, but one month of data was not sufficient to establish that the new arrangement would work equally well during exam periods or before holidays. A service like this should not be evaluated only by its total equipment capacity; the more important question is whether that capacity is available when demand is highest.

### Useful structures and collocations

**1. “增加容量”**

Use **increase capacity**, **expand capacity**, or **add capacity**.

Do not automatically use *increase the amount of equipment*. Capacity describes what a system can handle, not merely how many objects it contains.

**2. “集中送达”**

Useful choices:

- **arrive within a short time window**
- **be concentrated in the early evening**
- **a surge in deliveries**

**3. “柜格占用率”**

Use **locker occupancy rate** or **compartment occupancy rate**.

**4. “停留时间”**

In logistics and operations, **dwell time** is useful for how long an item remains in a location before moving on.

**5. “错峰”**

Depending on context:

- **stagger deliveries**
- **spread demand across time**
- **shift activity away from peak hours**

### Common Chinese-English transfer errors

1. **“快递”** in this context is usually **parcel / package / delivery**, not *express* by itself.
2. **“大件”** can be **oversized parcels/items**, not *big goods*.
3. **“晚高峰”** can be **the evening peak / peak evening period**, not *night rush*.
4. **“有所缩短”** is often **became shorter / was reduced somewhat**, not *had a certain reduction*.
5. **“还不足以说明”** is naturally **is not sufficient to establish/show that**, not *cannot explain that*.

## Exercise 2 — TOEFL / academic level

### Topic: evaluating extended library hours

Translate the passage before opening the answer.

**一所大学把主图书馆的闭馆时间从晚上十点延长到午夜，并计划根据第一学期的数据决定是否长期保留这一安排。表面上看，只要午夜前仍有大量学生留在馆内，就可以认为延长开放时间“有需求”。然而，这种观察本身并不能回答政策是否改善了学生的学习条件。首先，延长开放时间可能只是把原本在宿舍、自习室或咖啡馆学习的学生转移到了图书馆，而没有增加他们的总学习时间。其次，使用午夜时段的学生可能本来就与其他学生不同，例如他们的课程安排更晚、通勤时间更长，或者更偏好夜间学习。因此，如果学校想评估政策效果，就不能只比较延长前后的进馆人数。更有说服力的分析还应考虑座位利用率、学生原本会在哪里学习、工作人员和安保成本，以及不同学生群体是否同等受益。即使这些指标有所改善，也应避免把一个学期的变化直接解释为长期因果效果。**

Try to use:

- **at first glance**
- **indicate demand**
- **by itself**
- **shift activity from A to B**
- **differ systematically from**
- **a more convincing evaluation**
- **benefit equally**
- **long-term causal effect**

> [!answer]- Reference translation
> A university extended the closing time of its main library from 10 p.m. to midnight and planned to use data from the first semester to decide whether the change should become permanent. At first glance, if large numbers of students are still in the building before midnight, the university might reasonably conclude that there is demand for the additional hours. That observation by itself, however, does not show whether the policy has actually improved students' study conditions. First, longer opening hours may simply shift activity from dormitories, study rooms, or cafés to the library without increasing students' total study time. Second, students who use the late-night hours may differ systematically from other students: they may have later class schedules, longer commutes, or a stronger preference for studying at night. The university should therefore evaluate more than changes in entry counts before and after the extension. A more convincing evaluation would also consider seat utilization, where students would otherwise have studied, staffing and security costs, and whether different groups of students benefit equally. Even if several indicators improve, changes observed during a single semester should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of a long-term causal effect.

### Academic structures worth reusing

**1. at first glance**

Use this when an interpretation looks plausible initially but requires qualification.

> At first glance, the higher participation rate appears to indicate success.

**2. X by itself does not show / establish Y**

This is a strong academic pattern for separating one observation from a larger conclusion.

> A higher download count by itself does not establish that users benefited from the tool.

**3. shift activity from A to B**

Useful when an intervention changes location or timing rather than total quantity.

> The policy may shift traffic from one route to another rather than reduce total traffic.

**4. differ systematically from**

Use this when groups may have underlying differences that affect comparison.

> Volunteers may differ systematically from people who choose not to participate.

**5. would otherwise have**

Useful for counterfactual reasoning.

> Researchers asked where participants would otherwise have studied.

### Common transfer errors

1. **“有需求”** → **there is demand for... / indicate demand**, not *have needs*.
2. **“总学习时间”** → **total study time**, not *whole learning time*.
3. **“本来就不同”** → **already differ / differ systematically**, not *originally are different*.
4. **“更有说服力”** → **more convincing**, not *more persuasive power*.
5. **“同等受益”** → **benefit equally / receive comparable benefits**, not *get the same benefit absolutely*.
6. **“因果效果”** → **causal effect**, not *cause-and-effect result*.

## Precision rewrite — weaken claims to match the evidence

Rewrite each sentence into a more defensible version.

1. More parcel lockers eliminate pickup congestion.
2. Students staying until midnight proves the library extension improves learning.
3. A shorter queue means the system is fully optimized.
4. People who use late-night library hours are representative of all students.
5. One semester of better indicators shows the policy will work permanently.

Try to use:

- **may reduce**
- **is consistent with**
- **suggests rather than proves**
- **may differ from**
- **cannot yet establish**
- **under the conditions observed**

> [!answer]- Suggested rewrites
> 1. Additional parcel-locker capacity **may reduce** pickup congestion when a shortage of available compartments is an important bottleneck.
> 2. Continued use of the library before midnight **is consistent with demand for later hours, but does not by itself prove** that the extension improves learning outcomes.
> 3. A shorter queue **suggests an operational improvement under the conditions observed**, but it does not show that the system is fully optimized.
> 4. Students who use late-night hours **may differ systematically from** the wider student population.
> 5. Better indicators over one semester **cannot yet establish** that the policy will remain effective over the long term.

## Micro-drill — Chinese phrases that deserve restructuring

Translate naturally.

1. “并不是数量越多越好。”
2. “真正的瓶颈出现在需求最集中的时候。”
3. “这个变化可能只是把活动从一个地点转移到了另一个地点。”
4. “这些学生不一定能代表整个学生群体。”
5. “现有数据支持继续试点，但还不足以得出长期结论。”

> [!answer]- Suggested answers
> 1. **More is not necessarily better.**
> 2. **The real bottleneck appears when demand is most concentrated.**
> 3. **The change may simply shift activity from one location to another.**
> 4. **These students are not necessarily representative of the student population as a whole.**
> 5. **The existing data support continuing the pilot, but they are not yet sufficient for a long-term conclusion.**

## Spaced review — earlier language

### Review 1

**一个工具可以弥合部分使用差距，但仅仅提供工具并不能保证长期采用。**

> [!answer]- Suggested answer
> A tool can **bridge part of the gap** in access or usability, but making the tool available **does not by itself guarantee** long-term adoption.

### Review 2

**增加资源如果只能带来很小的边际改善，就应该重新考虑资源如何分配。**

> [!answer]- Suggested answer
> If additional resources produce only a **marginal improvement**, it may be worth reconsidering how those resources are **allocated**.
