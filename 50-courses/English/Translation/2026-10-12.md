## Exercise 1 — CET-6 level (15–18 minutes)

### Topic: clearer promotional labels in supermarkets

一家连锁超市发现，顾客经常误解货架上的促销标签。有些人以为“第二件半价”适用于所有商品，结账时才发现只有指定品牌参加活动。超市最初打算增加更多说明文字，但顾客调查显示，问题并非信息不足，而是关键信息被小字和复杂的条件遮住了。于是，门店开始把适用范围、活动期限和最终价格放在更醒目的位置，并让收银员记录仍然容易引起误解的标签。几周后，相关投诉有所减少，不过管理者没有立即认定新设计已经解决了问题，因为同期还调整了商品摆放方式。下一步，他们计划比较不同门店的数据，并询问顾客能否在购买前准确说出折扣条件。

> [!answer]- Reference translation — CET-6
> A supermarket chain found that customers often misunderstood promotional labels on its shelves. Some assumed that a “half price for the second item” offer applied to every product, only to discover at checkout that the promotion covered selected brands. The chain initially planned to add more explanatory text, but customer surveys suggested that the problem was not a lack of information. Rather, the key details were obscured by small print and complicated conditions. Stores therefore began displaying eligibility, the promotion period, and the final price more prominently. Cashiers were also asked to record labels that continued to cause confusion. A few weeks later, complaints about these offers had declined. However, managers did not immediately conclude that the new design had solved the problem, since product placement had also been changed during the same period. As a next step, they planned to compare data across stores and ask customers whether they could accurately identify the discount conditions before making a purchase.

### What the translation is doing

- **found that customers often misunderstood …** reports a recurring observation without claiming every customer was confused.
- **assumed that … applied to …** captures a mistaken interpretation. *Apply to* means “be relevant or valid for.”
- **the problem was not A; rather, B** corrects an initial diagnosis.
- **display information more prominently** is more idiomatic than *put information in a more obvious position* in formal service writing.
- **complaints had declined** describes a change in the observed count. It does not establish why the count fell.

### Common Chinese → English transfer errors

1. “顾客经常误解” → **customers often misunderstood the labels**, not *customers often had misunderstanding to the labels*.
2. “适用于所有商品” → **apply to all products / cover all products**, not *be suitable to all products*.
3. “结账时才发现” → **only to discover at checkout that …** expresses an unexpected result.
4. “问题并非信息不足” → **the problem was not a lack of information**, not *the problem was not information insufficient*.
5. “投诉有所减少” → **complaints declined / there were fewer complaints**, not *the complaints were reduced* unless someone actively reduced them.
6. If product placement changed at the same time as the labels, the decline cannot automatically be attributed to the labels alone. Preserve the concession introduced by “不过”.

## Exercise 2 — TOEFL / academic level (20–22 minutes)

### Topic: who maintains open-source scientific software?

越来越多的研究团队依赖公开的软件包来处理数据、绘制图表和复现分析流程。由于这些工具通常可以免费获取，研究人员有时会把它们视为几乎没有维护成本的公共资源。然而，软件能否长期可靠地使用，取决于是否有人修复缺陷、更新依赖项、编写文档，并在操作系统或数据格式发生变化时进行适配。维护工作往往不如发布新功能容易获得认可，因此贡献者可能在项目变得重要之后逐渐减少投入。若资助机构只根据论文数量或新功能的数量评估软件项目，就可能低估那些用于测试、兼容性和用户支持的劳动。更合理的评估方式应同时考虑使用范围、问题响应时间、版本稳定性和社区交接能力。即使某个工具目前被广泛使用，也不能据此推断它未来一定会得到维护；长期可持续性需要明确的责任安排和持续的资源支持。

> [!answer]- Reference translation — TOEFL / academic English
> An increasing number of research teams rely on openly available software packages to process data, generate figures, and reproduce analytical workflows. Because these tools can often be obtained free of charge, researchers may treat them as public resources that require little or no maintenance. In reality, their long-term reliability depends on whether someone corrects defects, updates dependencies, documents the code, and adapts the software when operating systems or data formats change. Maintenance work is often less visible and less likely to receive recognition than the release of new features. As a result, contributors may gradually reduce their involvement after a project becomes widely used. If funding agencies evaluate software projects solely by publication counts or the number of new features, they may underestimate the labor required for testing, compatibility, and user support. A more defensible evaluation would consider usage, response times for reported problems, release stability, and the community's ability to transfer responsibility. Widespread use today does not guarantee that a tool will continue to be maintained. Long-term sustainability requires clearly assigned responsibilities and ongoing resources.

### Academic structures worth reusing

- **rely on A to do B / rely on A for B** — describe functional dependence.
- **depend on whether …** — introduces a condition that must be satisfied, not a guaranteed outcome.
- **be less likely to receive recognition than …** — a precise comparative structure.
- **If A, B may …** — a cautious conditional that avoids claiming the consequence always occurs.
- **solely by / alongside / take A into account** — useful when explaining why one metric is insufficient.
- **does not guarantee that …** — separates present evidence from future certainty.
- **require clearly assigned responsibilities and ongoing resources** — concise institutional language for continuity.

### Common transfer errors in academic writing

1. “免费获取” → **available free of charge / freely available**, not *can be got for free* in formal prose.
2. “依赖项” in software → **dependencies**, not *dependences*.
3. “进行适配” → **adapt the software to changes in …** or **make the software compatible with …**, not *do adaptation*.
4. “低估劳动” → **underestimate the labor/work involved**, not *look down on the labor*.
5. “广泛使用” → **widely used / widespread use**; it does not prove reliability or future maintenance.
6. “责任安排” → **an allocation of responsibilities / clearly assigned responsibilities**.
7. “持续的资源支持” → **ongoing resource support / sustained funding and staffing**.

## Precision rewrite — strengthen the logic, not the rhetoric

Rewrite each sentence so that its claim matches the evidence. Do not solve every problem by adding *maybe*.

1. The new labels reduced complaints, so they were definitely the only reason customers became less confused.
2. Open-source software is free, so maintaining it costs nothing.
3. A software package with many users will always be maintained.
4. The more features a program has, the better it is.

> [!answer]- Suggested precision rewrites
> 1. Complaints declined after the labels were redesigned, but the simultaneous change in product placement makes it difficult to attribute the entire decline to the labels.
> 2. Open-source software may be free to obtain, but maintaining it still requires time, testing, documentation, and technical support.
> 3. A large user base may encourage continued maintenance, but continuity also depends on active contributors, clear responsibilities, and resources.
> 4. Additional features can expand a program's capabilities, but their value depends on reliability, usability, and users' needs.
