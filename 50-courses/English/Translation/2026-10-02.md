## Exercise 1 — CET-6 level

### Topic: shared umbrellas near subway stations

Translate before opening the answer.

**某城市在几个地铁站出口设置了共享雨伞柜，希望乘客在突然下雨时可以短时间借用雨伞。项目刚开始时，运营方主要关注投放了多少把伞，但很快发现，仅仅增加数量并不能解决所有问题。有些站点早高峰借出很多雨伞，却因为附近归还点太少，晚上几乎没有伞可用；另一些站点虽然库存充足，但设备位置不明显，很多乘客根本没有注意到。雨伞损坏、归还信息更新不及时以及连续几天降雨也会影响实际可用数量。因此，运营方开始同时记录借出率、按时归还率、损坏率和不同时间段的库存变化，并调整柜机位置。一个共享服务是否好用，不能只看总投放量，还要看用户在真正需要时能否方便地找到并使用它。**

Try to use: **deploy, availability, return rate, inventory, cannot be judged solely by**.

> [!answer]- Reference translation
> A city installed shared-umbrella cabinets near the exits of several subway stations so that passengers could borrow umbrellas for short periods when rain began unexpectedly. At the start of the program, the operator focused mainly on how many umbrellas had been deployed. It soon became clear, however, that simply increasing the total number did not solve every problem. Some stations lent out many umbrellas during the morning rush hour but had almost none available in the evening because there were too few nearby return points. Other stations had sufficient inventory, yet the cabinets were easy to overlook, so many passengers did not notice them. Damaged umbrellas, delays in updating return information, and several consecutive rainy days could also reduce actual availability. The operator therefore began tracking borrowing rates, on-time return rates, damage rates, and changes in inventory at different times of day, while also adjusting cabinet locations. The quality of a shared service cannot be judged solely by the total number of items deployed; it also depends on whether users can find and use them conveniently when they actually need them.

### Useful structures / transfer errors

- **deploy umbrellas / install cabinets** — 投放雨伞 / 设置柜机
- **during the morning rush hour** — 早高峰期间
- **have sufficient inventory** — 库存充足
- **be easy to overlook** — 位置不明显、容易被忽略
- **actual availability** — 实际可用性
- **on-time return rate** — 按时归还率
- **adjust the placement of** — 调整……的位置
- **cannot be judged solely by** — 不能只根据……判断

Avoid literal forms such as *returning place*, *umbrella amount*, and *whether it is good to use*.

## Exercise 2 — TOEFL / academic level

### Topic: urban tree cover and summer heat

Translate with careful attention to measurement, confounding, and scope.

**许多城市希望通过增加树木覆盖来缓解夏季高温。一项观察性研究如果发现树木较多的街区平均地表温度较低，这一结果可以支持进一步研究，但不能直接证明“种树本身导致了全部降温”。树木较多的街区可能同时拥有更大的公园、更低的建筑密度、更少的机动车交通，甚至不同的地形和收入水平，而这些因素也可能与温度有关。此外，卫星测得的地表温度并不等同于人在街道上实际承受的热暴露：阴影、湿度、风速和夜间散热都会影响人体感受。更有说服力的研究可以比较同一地点在植树前后的变化，设置合适的对照区域，并记录空气温度、地表温度和行人热暴露等多种指标。即使结果显示树木覆盖与降温稳定相关，研究者仍需要说明这种关系在不同气候、街道形态和树种条件下是否同样成立。**

Preserve:
- association vs. causation;
- surface temperature vs. human heat exposure;
- local finding vs. generalization.

> [!answer]- Reference translation
> Many cities hope to reduce summer heat by increasing tree cover. If an observational study finds that neighborhoods with more trees have lower average land-surface temperatures, the result can justify further investigation, but it does not directly establish that tree planting itself caused all of the observed cooling. Neighborhoods with greater tree cover may also have larger parks, lower building density, less motor-vehicle traffic, or different topography and income levels, any of which may also be related to temperature. In addition, land-surface temperature measured by satellite is not the same as the heat exposure experienced by people at street level. Shade, humidity, wind speed, and nighttime heat release can all affect thermal conditions. A more persuasive study could compare the same locations before and after tree planting, include suitable control areas, and record multiple outcomes such as air temperature, surface temperature, and pedestrian heat exposure. Even if tree cover remains consistently associated with cooling, researchers should still state whether the relationship is likely to hold across different climates, street forms, and tree species.

### Academic language

- **mitigate summer heat**
- **tree-canopy cover**
- **observational study**
- **be associated with**
- **does not directly establish that**
- **land-surface temperature**
- **street-level heat exposure**
- **before-and-after comparison**
- **control area**
- **record multiple outcomes**
- **hold across different settings**

Common errors:
- *tree covering rate* → **tree cover / tree-canopy cover**
- *solve high temperature* → **reduce / mitigate heat**
- *observation study* → **observational study**
- *hot exposure* → **heat exposure**
- *contrast area* → **control area / comparison area**

## Precision rewrite

Rewrite each claim more carefully.

1. **Adding more umbrellas will solve the availability problem.**
2. **Trees make neighborhoods cooler.**
3. **Satellite data show how hot pedestrians feel.**
4. **A high return rate proves the umbrella service is convenient.**

> [!answer]- Suggested rewrites
> 1. Adding more umbrellas may improve availability, but placement, return behavior, maintenance, and weather can also affect whether umbrellas are available when needed.
> 2. Neighborhoods with greater tree cover may be cooler on average, but observational comparisons alone cannot determine how much of the difference is caused by the trees themselves.
> 3. Satellite surface-temperature data provide useful information about thermal conditions, but they do not directly measure the heat exposure experienced by pedestrians at street level.
> 4. A high return rate is useful evidence about system operation, but it does not by itself establish that users can find umbrellas easily when they need them.
