## Exercise 1 — CET-6 level (15 minutes)

### Topic: Independent bookstores as community spaces

Translate the entire paragraph. Preserve the concession, the operating constraints, and the balanced conclusion.

一家开在老城区的独立书店近来尝试延长周末营业时间，并在晚上举办读书会、小型讲座和作者交流活动。店主希望让书店不再只是购买图书的地方，也成为附近居民愿意反复走进来的公共空间。最初几场活动吸引了不少年轻人，社交媒体上的照片也带来了关注，但热闹并不一定能转化为稳定的收入。延长营业时间意味着额外的人工和照明成本，而活动参与者也未必会买书。为了避免仅凭人气判断经营状况，书店开始分别记录到店人数、图书销售额、活动报名后的实际出席率，以及第一次参加活动的顾客是否再次光顾。店主认为，书店可以承担文化交流的功能，但要把这件事长期做好，就必须在公共价值和日常经营之间找到可持续的平衡。

**Target collocations:** extend opening hours; host a book club; attract attention; translate into revenue; incur costs; actual attendance; make a return visit; strike a balance between.

> [!answer]- Reference translation — CET-6
> An independent bookstore in an old neighborhood has recently experimented with extending its weekend opening hours and hosting evening book clubs, small talks, and meet-the-author events. The owner hopes to turn the store into more than a place where people buy books: it could also become a public space that nearby residents want to return to. The first few events attracted many young visitors, and photographs shared on social media drew further attention. Yet a lively atmosphere does not necessarily translate into steady revenue. Longer opening hours incur additional staffing and lighting costs, and people who attend an event do not always purchase books. Rather than judging performance by popularity alone, the store has begun tracking foot traffic, book sales, actual attendance among people who registered for events, and whether first-time participants make return visits. The owner believes that a bookstore can serve as a space for cultural exchange, but sustaining that role requires a workable balance between public value and day-to-day commercial operations.

### What the English structures do

| Chinese meaning | Better English | Why this is useful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 不再只是 A，也是 B | **more than A; also B** | Express a second role concisely. |
| 热闹不一定能转化为收入 | **a lively atmosphere does not necessarily translate into revenue** | State a non-guaranteed relationship. |
| 额外的人工费用 | **additional staffing costs** | **Staffing** refers to employing people; avoid literal “personnel expenses” in casual reports. |
| 报名后的出席率 | **attendance among registrants** | Registration is not attendance. |
| 再次光顾 | **make a return visit** | Natural in business and service evaluation. |
| 两者之间的平衡 | **a balance between A and B** | Name both competing objectives. |

### Chinese → English transfer traps

1. **“prolong the weekend business time”** sounds unnatural. Use **extend weekend opening hours**.
2. **“catch young people's eyes”** is an overly literal expression. Use **attract young visitors / draw attention**.
3. **“hot atmosphere makes stable income”** omits the crucial uncertainty. Use **does not necessarily translate into**.
4. **“come a second time”** is grammatical but clumsy. Prefer **return / make a return visit**.
5. **“bookstore has culture function”** becomes **the bookstore can serve a cultural function** or **serve as a space for cultural exchange**.

**Self-check:** Did you retain the difference among attention, visits, participation, purchasing, and revenue? Do not treat five distinct measures as synonyms.

## Exercise 2 — TOEFL / academic level (20 minutes)

### Topic: Evaluating improvisation in music education

Translate the argument without turning hypotheses into proven results.

在音乐教育中，越来越多的教师尝试让学生在掌握基本节奏和音阶之后进行即兴演奏，而不是始终按照乐谱重复练习。支持者认为，即兴演奏迫使学生主动倾听、预测其他演奏者的反应，并在有限的时间内作出选择，因此可能有助于提高音乐表达能力。然而，课堂气氛变得活跃，并不能直接证明学生的音乐能力得到了提高。愿意参加即兴活动的学生，可能原本就拥有更丰富的演奏经验，或者更不害怕在同伴面前犯错。若研究者只比较活动前后的满意度问卷，就难以区分教学方法的效果与学生原有差异的影响。更严谨的研究可以在课程开始前记录学生的基础水平，设置练习总时长相近的对照组，并让不了解分组情况的评委按照统一标准评价演奏。即便这样的研究发现了积极结果，也仍需考察效果是否能延续到下一个学期，以及是否适用于不同年龄和学习背景的学生。教育创新值得尝试，但其价值应由清晰的学习目标与可靠证据来判断，而不是仅由课堂是否热闹来决定。

**Target academic moves:** plausible mechanism; do not by themselves establish; pre-existing differences; comparison group; blind to group assignment; persist over time; generalize across.

> [!answer]- Reference translation — TOEFL / academic
> In music education, a growing number of teachers are experimenting with improvisation after students have acquired basic rhythms and scales, rather than requiring them to practice exclusively by repeating what is written in a score. Advocates argue that improvisation requires students to listen actively, anticipate other performers' responses, and make decisions under time constraints. These demands could plausibly strengthen musical expression. However, a more lively classroom does not by itself demonstrate an improvement in students' musical ability. Students who choose to participate in improvisational activities may already have more performance experience or feel less anxious about making mistakes in front of their peers. If researchers compare only satisfaction questionnaires collected before and after the activity, they may be unable to distinguish the effects of the teaching method from pre-existing differences among students. A more rigorous study could assess students' starting abilities, include a comparison group with a similar total amount of practice, and have judges who are blind to group assignment evaluate performances using consistent criteria. Even if such a study found positive effects, researchers would still need to investigate whether the benefits persist into the following semester and generalize to students of different ages and educational backgrounds. Educational innovation deserves consideration, but its value should be judged by clear learning objectives and reliable evidence, not simply by how energetic a classroom appears.

### Academic patterns worth collecting

**Hypothesized mechanism:** **X could plausibly improve Y by requiring students to ...** signals a proposed explanation, not established causality.

**Selection effect:** **Participants may differ systematically from nonparticipants.** Use **pre-existing differences** for group differences present before an intervention.

**Measurement problem:** **A satisfaction rating does not by itself establish improved performance.** Satisfaction and learning outcomes are separate constructs.

**Study design:** **include a comparison group with comparable practice time**; **have evaluators who are blind to group assignment**. A properly designed comparison can make a claim stronger, but no single design eliminates every limitation.

**Generalization:** **Even if the intervention improves performance in this setting, it remains unclear whether the effect will persist or generalize to other groups.**

### Frequent transfer errors

- **“free playing”** is vague; use **improvisation / improvise**.
- **“improve the music level of students”** is unnatural; use **improve students' musical ability / performance**.
- **“original difference of students”** should be **pre-existing differences / differences at baseline**.
- **“control team”** should be **comparison group / control group**.
- **“blind judges”** could mean visually impaired judges. Say **judges blind to group assignment**.
- **“prove effective next semester”** makes an unwarranted prediction. Say **examine whether the effects persist into the next semester**.

## Precision rewrite — five overstatements

Rewrite each English sentence more carefully and naturally.

1. **All the readers liked the event, so the bookstore was financially successful.**
2. **Improvisation made the class energetic, proving that students learned more.**
3. **The questionnaire controlled for every difference between students.**
4. **This policy is useful because it has no costs.**
5. **The result applies to all schools because it worked at one college.**

> [!answer]- Suggested precision rewrites
> 1. Positive responses may improve the store's visibility, but they do not necessarily indicate stable revenue.
> 2. Greater classroom engagement may be promising, but it does not by itself demonstrate improved musical ability.
> 3. The questionnaire measured reported satisfaction but did not control for pre-existing differences.
> 4. The policy may produce benefits, although implementation and maintenance involve costs.
> 5. Results from one college may not generalize to schools serving different student populations.
