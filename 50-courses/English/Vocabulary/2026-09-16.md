# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-16

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-16|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-16|Today's translation]]

Today's set focuses on language for **temporary systems, flexible design, adaptation, and practical constraints**. Several items connect naturally to the TED talk, but all of them transfer well to engineering, policy, project work, and academic writing.

## 1. ephemeral

**Meaning:** 短暂的；存在时间很短的

### Collocations
- ephemeral phenomenon
- ephemeral popularity
- ephemeral settlement
- ephemeral experience

### Example
> The installation was intentionally ephemeral: it existed for only a few weeks and left almost no permanent structure behind.

### Contrast
- **temporary** is neutral and practical: something is not permanent.
- **ephemeral** emphasizes how brief or fleeting something is and often sounds more literary or academic.

### Reliable memory note
The word ultimately comes from Greek **ephēmeros**, meaning “lasting only a day.” That original sense helps explain why modern English uses **ephemeral** for things that disappear quickly.

## 2. assemble / disassemble

**Meaning:** 组装 / 拆卸

### Collocations
- assemble a structure
- assemble components
- be assembled on site
- disassemble equipment
- designed for disassembly

### Word family
- **assemble** (v.)
- **assembly** (n.)
- **disassemble** (v.)
- **disassembly** (n.)

### Example
> The stage can be assembled in a few hours and disassembled after the event.

### Contrast
**dismantle** is close to **disassemble**, but often suggests taking apart a larger structure, institution, or system. **Disassemble** is especially natural when something consists of components that can be separated.

## 3. modular

**Meaning:** 模块化的；由可组合、替换的独立单元构成的

### Collocations
- modular design
- modular system
- modular construction
- modular components

### Word family
- **module** (n.)
- **modular** (adj.)
- **modularity** (n.)

### Example
> A modular design allows damaged components to be replaced without rebuilding the entire system.

### Transfer value
This word is useful in architecture, software, electronics, education, and experimental design.

## 4. accommodate

**Meaning:** 容纳；适应；满足某种需求

### Collocations
- accommodate large numbers of people
- accommodate demand
- accommodate different needs
- accommodate changes in schedule

### Example
> The hall was reconfigured to accommodate a much larger audience than usual.

### Common error
Do not reduce **accommodate** to “provide a room for.” In academic and professional English, it often means **adjusting a system so that it can handle a need or constraint**.

## 5. reversible / reversibility

**Meaning:** 可逆的；能够恢复到先前状态的 / 可逆性

### Collocations
- reversible change
- reversible process
- reversible design
- degree of reversibility

### Example
> Reversible changes are valuable when future needs are uncertain because they preserve the option to return to an earlier configuration.

### Contrast
- **reversible** = can be undone or returned to an earlier state
- **repeatable** = can be performed again with similar results

A process may be repeatable without being reversible.

## 6. adapt to / adaptable

**Meaning:** 适应…… / 适应性强的

### Collocations
- adapt to changing conditions
- adapt to new constraints
- highly adaptable system
- adaptable to different uses

### Word family
- **adapt** (v.)
- **adaptation** (n.)
- **adaptable** (adj.)
- **adaptive** (adj.)

### Usage note
**adaptable** usually describes something capable of being adjusted. **Adaptive** often describes a system or behavior that actively changes in response to conditions.

### Example
> An adaptable workspace can support lectures, group discussions, and exhibitions without major reconstruction.

## 7. repurpose

**Meaning:** 改作他用；赋予已有物品或空间新的用途

### Collocations
- repurpose a building
- repurpose existing infrastructure
- repurpose materials
- be repurposed for another use

### Example
> An unused parking area can sometimes be repurposed as a weekend market or event space.

### Contrast
- **reuse** = use again, often for the same or a similar purpose
- **repurpose** = give something a different purpose
- **recycle** = process material so it can become raw material for new products

## 8. footprint

**Meaning:** 足迹；比喻义常指一个活动、建筑或系统占用的空间、资源或造成的环境影响

### Collocations
- physical footprint
- environmental footprint
- carbon footprint
- reduce one's footprint
- leave a light footprint

### Example
> Temporary infrastructure can reduce the physical footprint of facilities that are needed only a few weeks each year.

### Usage caution
**Footprint** is useful but broad. In precise writing, specify whether you mean **land use, energy consumption, carbon emissions, material use,** or another measurable impact.

## 9. constraint

**Meaning:** 限制条件；约束

### Collocations
- budget constraint
- time constraint
- physical constraint
- work within constraints
- impose a constraint on...

### Word family
- **constrain** (v.)
- **constraint** (n.)
- **constrained** (adj.)

### Example
> Good design often comes from finding workable solutions within strict cost and space constraints.

### Contrast
- **constraint** = a condition that limits available choices
- **obstacle** = something that blocks progress and may need to be overcome
- **limitation** = a broader term for weakness, boundary, or restriction

## Useful mini-patterns

### Design under uncertainty
> The system is **designed to accommodate** changes in demand rather than assuming that current conditions will remain fixed.

### Trade-off
> A modular structure offers greater flexibility, **but this comes at the cost of** additional setup and maintenance.

### Cautious application
> The approach may be useful **under certain constraints**, but it should not be treated as a universal solution.

### Resource use
> Existing infrastructure can be **repurposed for** temporary needs instead of being replaced entirely.
