# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-07

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-07|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-07|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary is partly connected to the Harvard Gazette interview, but the goal is broader: reusable English for **openness, restraint, rapport, reciprocity, boundaries, and socially calibrated communication**.

## 1. open up

**Meaning:** 敞开心扉；开始更坦率地谈自己的想法、感受或经历。

### Collocations
- open up to someone
- gradually open up
- find it hard to open up
- encourage someone to open up
- open up about a problem

### Example
> Students may open up more once they feel that a discussion is respectful and confidential.

### Contrast
**Open up** suggests becoming more forthcoming. It does not mean telling someone everything.

---

## 2. hold back

**Meaning:** 克制、不说出、保留；也可表示阻碍。

### Collocations
- hold back a comment
- hold back information
- hold back from saying something
- hold back because of concern
- be held back by fear

### Example
> She held back from criticizing the proposal until she had checked the data.

### Contrast
**Hold back** can be deliberate and sensible. It is not automatically the same as being dishonest.

---

## 3. build rapport

**Meaning:** 建立融洽、互信而自然的互动关系。

### Collocations
- build rapport with
- establish rapport
- develop rapport
- quickly build rapport
- rapport between colleagues

### Example
> Brief informal conversation can help build rapport before a difficult meeting.

### Usage note
**Rapport** is pronounced approximately /ræˈpɔːr/. The final **t** is silent.

---

## 4. come across as

**Meaning:** 给人某种印象；显得像……

### Collocations
- come across as confident
- come across as defensive
- come across as dismissive
- come across as sincere
- come across as overly formal

### Example
> A very short reply may come across as unfriendly even when the writer only intended to be efficient.

### Contrast
**Be** describes what something is. **Come across as** describes how it is perceived.

---

## 5. reciprocate

**Meaning:** 回应、回报；以类似方式回应对方的行为、态度或情感。

### Collocations
- reciprocate trust
- reciprocate interest
- reciprocate a gesture
- reciprocate someone's openness
- reciprocal relationship

### Example
> When one speaker asks thoughtful questions, the other person is often more willing to reciprocate.

### Word family
- **reciprocal** (adj.)
- **reciprocity** (n.)

### Contrast
**Respond** is general. **Reciprocate** implies giving something comparable back.

---

## 6. read the room

**Meaning:** 观察现场气氛、他人的反应和社会线索，并据此调整自己的表达。

### Collocations
- fail to read the room
- know how to read the room
- read the room before speaking
- read the mood of the room

### Example
> Before making a joke during a tense discussion, it helps to read the room.

### Register
This is common spoken and professional English. In formal writing, alternatives include **assess the social context** or **respond to contextual cues**.

---

## 7. put someone on the spot

**Meaning:** 让某人突然陷入必须立即回答、表态或做决定的尴尬压力中。

### Collocations
- put someone on the spot
- feel put on the spot
- avoid putting someone on the spot
- a question that puts someone on the spot

### Example
> Asking for an immediate commitment in front of the whole group may put a colleague on the spot.

### Contrast
**Challenge someone** can be intentional and direct. **Put someone on the spot** emphasizes social pressure and lack of preparation time.

---

## 8. confide in

**Meaning:** 向某人吐露较私密或重要的信息，因为你信任对方。

### Collocations
- confide in a friend
- confide in a colleague
- feel comfortable confiding in someone
- have no one to confide in

### Example
> People are more likely to confide in someone who has shown that they can listen without immediately judging.

### Contrast
**Tell someone** is neutral. **Confide in someone** implies trust and usually some degree of sensitivity.

---

## 9. draw a boundary

**Meaning:** 划定界限；明确什么可以接受、什么不愿继续。

### Collocations
- draw a clear boundary
- draw boundaries around work
- set and maintain boundaries
- respect a boundary
- cross a boundary

### Example
> It is possible to be polite while drawing a clear boundary around questions you do not want to discuss.

### Contrast
**Draw / set a boundary** establishes the limit. **Maintain a boundary** means continuing to enforce it over time.

## Reusable mini-patterns

> It took time for the group to **open up**.

> I **held back** until I understood the situation better.

> A few minutes of informal conversation helped us **build rapport**.

> The message **came across as** more critical than intended.

> Trust often grows when openness is **reciprocated**.

> You need to **read the room** before changing the tone.

> I did not want to **put her on the spot** in front of everyone.

> He chose to **confide in** a close colleague.

> You can disagree while still **drawing a clear boundary**.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise expression.

1. You gradually become more willing to discuss what is bothering you: **open up / shut down**.
2. You decide not to criticize a draft until you have more evidence: **hold back / blurt out**.
3. You create an easy, trusting conversational connection: **build rapport / create distance**.
4. Your email unintentionally seems cold: it **comes across as / objectively is** cold.
5. Someone shares trust and you respond with comparable trust: **reciprocate / ignore**.
6. You notice the group is tense and adjust your tone: **read the room / miss the point**.
7. You ask a difficult question publicly and the person must answer immediately: **put them on the spot / give them space**.
8. You share something important with a trusted person: **confide in / broadcast to** them.
9. You politely say a topic is not open for discussion: **draw a boundary / erase a boundary**.

## Precision pairs

### open up vs. overshare

**Open up** describes increased willingness to share.  
**Overshare** evaluates the amount or appropriateness of what was shared.

You can open up without oversharing.

### hold back vs. hide

**Hold back** may be strategic, temporary, or polite.  
**Hide** more strongly suggests deliberately keeping something from view.

### rapport vs. agreement

You can have excellent **rapport** with someone and still disagree with them.

### reciprocity vs. symmetry

**Reciprocity** means mutual response. It does not require both people to behave identically.

### boundary vs. barrier

A **boundary** defines a limit. A **barrier** blocks access or movement more broadly.
