# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-21

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-21|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-21|Today's translation]]

Today's set partly connects to the NPS article, but the target is broader: reusable language for **describing conditions, measuring effects, managing problems, and discussing trade-offs**. The emphasis is on collocation and contrast rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

## 1. ambient

**Meaning:** 周围环境中的；背景性的。常用于声音、光线、温度、空气等不是由一个瞬间事件产生，而是构成整体环境条件的因素。

### Collocations
- ambient noise
- ambient light
- ambient temperature
- ambient conditions
- ambient air

### Example
> The sensor records ambient noise for several hours so that researchers can distinguish the normal background level from unusual events.

### Contrast
- **background** is broader and more everyday.
- **ambient** is common in technical, environmental, engineering, and design contexts and usually refers to the surrounding physical environment.

## 2. intrusive

**Meaning:** 侵扰性的；过度闯入、打扰或影响本不应被打扰的空间、活动或体验。

### Collocations
- intrusive noise
- intrusive lighting
- intrusive advertising
- intrusive questions
- feel intrusive

### Example
> Even relatively brief construction noise can feel intrusive when it repeatedly interrupts a task that requires concentration.

### Word family
- **intrude** (v.) — 闯入；侵扰
- **intrusion** (n.) — 侵入；干扰
- **intrusive** (adj.) — 侵扰性的

### Contrast
**disruptive** emphasizes the effect—something interrupts a process. **Intrusive** emphasizes unwanted entry into a space, activity, or experience.

## 3. mitigate

**Meaning:** 缓解；减轻某种负面影响、风险或严重程度，而不一定完全消除问题。

### Collocations
- mitigate risk
- mitigate the impact of...
- mitigation measures
- mitigate environmental damage
- mitigate adverse effects

### Example
> Better insulation can mitigate traffic noise without eliminating the source of the noise itself.

### Contrast
- **eliminate** = remove completely
- **reduce** = make smaller, very general
- **mitigate** = reduce the severity or harmful consequences, especially in policy, engineering, climate, health, and risk management

A common mistake is to treat **mitigate** as if it meant “solve.” It usually does not.

## 4. monitor

**Meaning:** 持续或定期监测，以观察变化、异常或长期趋势。

### Collocations
- monitor air quality
- monitor performance
- monitor changes over time
- continuous monitoring
- monitoring equipment

### Example
> The city installed sensors to monitor how noise levels change between rush hour and late evening.

### Contrast
- **measure** can be a single measurement.
- **monitor** normally implies repeated or continuous observation over time.

### Word family
- monitoring (n.)
- monitor (n./v.)

## 5. baseline

**Meaning:** 基线；开始比较、评估变化或判断效果时使用的参照水平。

### Collocations
- establish a baseline
- baseline data
- baseline performance
- compare against the baseline
- baseline conditions

### Example
> Without reliable baseline data, it is difficult to know whether the intervention produced a meaningful change.

### Usage note
A **baseline** is not simply an “average.” It is a reference point selected for comparison—often the condition before a policy, treatment, or intervention begins.

## 6. adjacent

**Meaning:** 相邻的；紧靠的。比 **nearby** 更精确，通常强调空间上直接相邻或非常接近。

### Collocations
- adjacent building
- adjacent community
- adjacent land
- adjacent to the park
- two adjacent rooms

### Example
> Lighting from an adjacent commercial district can affect the night sky even if the protected area itself uses very little artificial light.

### Contrast
- **nearby** = somewhere near; does not imply direct adjacency
- **adjacent** = next to or immediately beside

Grammar note: use **adjacent to**, not *adjacent with*.

## 7. spill over / spillover

**Meaning:** 影响越过原本的边界扩散到其他地区、群体或领域。作动词常写 **spill over into**；作名词常写 **spillover effect**。

### Collocations
- spill over into neighboring areas
- spillover effects
- economic spillovers
- negative / positive spillovers

### Example
> Congestion in one district can spill over into adjacent neighborhoods when drivers search for alternative routes.

### Contrast
**spread** is general. **Spill over** highlights that an effect crosses a boundary or extends beyond the place where it began.

## 8. trade-off

**Meaning:** 权衡；为了获得一种好处而接受另一种成本或损失的情况。

### Collocations
- a trade-off between A and B
- involve a trade-off
- manage a trade-off
- an unavoidable trade-off
- efficiency–equity trade-off

### Example
> Extending library hours may improve access, but it can involve a trade-off between convenience and staffing cost.

### Contrast
A **compromise** is an agreement in which sides make concessions. A **trade-off** is the underlying relationship in which improving one objective may make another harder to achieve.

## 9. cumulative

**Meaning:** 累积的；由许多较小影响随着时间或次数增加而形成的总效果。

### Collocations
- cumulative effect
- cumulative exposure
- cumulative damage
- cumulative total
- cumulative impact

### Example
> A single noisy event may be minor, but the cumulative effect of repeated disturbance can be much greater.

### Word family
- **accumulate** (v.) — 积累
- **accumulation** (n.) — 积累
- **cumulative** (adj.) — 累积形成的

### Contrast
**overall** means considering everything together. **Cumulative** specifically emphasizes effects building up over time or through repeated additions.

## Reusable mini-patterns

### Measurement before intervention
> Researchers first **established a baseline** and then **monitored** changes after the policy was introduced.

### Cross-boundary effect
> Pollution generated in an **adjacent** area can **spill over into** the protected zone.

### Reduce rather than eliminate
> The new design does not eliminate the problem, but it may **mitigate** its most serious effects.

### Cost-benefit qualification
> The policy involves a **trade-off between** easier access and greater operating cost.

### Repeated exposure
> The **cumulative impact** may be more important than any single short-term event.

## Contrast drill

Choose the better word and explain why.

1. Researchers took one reading / **monitored** the site for six months.
2. The new rule eliminated / **mitigated** the risk but did not remove it completely.
3. A restaurant five blocks away is adjacent / **nearby**.
4. The office next door is **adjacent** / nearby to the laboratory.
5. One brief exposure is minor, but the overall / **cumulative** effect of daily exposure may be substantial.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. **monitored** — repeated observation over time
> 2. **mitigated** — the risk was reduced, not removed
> 3. **nearby** — five blocks away is close but not directly next to the location
> 4. **adjacent** — the office is directly next to the laboratory; use **adjacent to**
> 5. **cumulative** — the effect builds through repeated exposure

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. ambient
> 2. mitigate
> 3. **Measure** can refer to one observation; **monitor** usually means repeated or continuous observation over time.
> 4. baseline
> 5. adjacent
> 6. spill

> 8. cumulative
> 9. Example: **intrusive lighting** that enters a bedroom at night; the emphasis is on unwanted intrusion into a space.
> 10. Example: The policy **mitigated** some of the negative effects.
