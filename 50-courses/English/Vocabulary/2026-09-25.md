# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-25

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-25|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-25|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary partly connects to the memory article, but the target is broader: reusable English for **remembering, interference, reconstruction, evidence limits, and overgeneralization**. Focus on collocations, word families, and contrasts.

## 1. retrieval cue

**Meaning:** 提取线索；能帮助记忆中的信息被重新想起的提示、环境特征或关联。

### Collocations
- a retrieval cue
- provide a retrieval cue
- depend on retrieval cues
- an effective retrieval cue
- cue-dependent recall

### Example
> A calendar notification can serve as a retrieval cue for a task that might otherwise be forgotten.

### Contrast
- **reminder** = 日常意义上的提醒，可以是人主动设置的
- **retrieval cue** = 更强调某个线索触发记忆提取这一认知过程

## 2. retain

**Meaning:** 保留；保持。学术语境中常用于“保留信息、能力、员工、热量、水分”等。

### Collocations
- retain information
- retain knowledge
- retain the ability to...
- retain heat / moisture
- retain employees
- information retention

### Example
> Students may retain more information when they actively retrieve it rather than simply rereading the same material.

### Word family
- **retention** (n.)

### Contrast
- **remember** = 记得，最普通
- **retain** = 强调信息在一段时间后仍被保存

## 3. impair

**Meaning:** 损害；削弱某种功能、能力或性能，但通常不是“完全摧毁”。

### Collocations
- impair performance
- impair memory
- impair judgment
- impair the ability to...
- cognitive impairment

### Example
> Severe noise can impair performance on tasks that require sustained concentration.

### Word family
- **impairment** (n.)

### Contrast
- **damage** = 更广泛，可用于物理损坏
- **impair** = 常用于功能或能力变差
- **prevent** = 阻止发生，语义比 impair 强

## 4. distraction

**Meaning:** 分心因素；注意力被拉走的状态或事物。

### Collocations
- a major distraction
- minimize distractions
- visual / auditory distractions
- be distracted by...
- distract attention from...
- distraction-free environment

### Example
> A notification may be a minor distraction once, but repeated interruptions can make a complex task harder to resume.

### Word family
- **distract** (v.)
- **distracted** (adj.)
- **distracting** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **interruption** = 某件事实际打断了正在进行的活动
- **distraction** = 任何把注意力拉走的因素，不一定造成完全中断

## 5. reconstruct

**Meaning:** 重建；重新构建。除了建筑和历史复原，在心理学和论证中也可表示根据零散信息重新形成一个完整版本。

### Collocations
- reconstruct an event
- reconstruct a sequence
- reconstruct the past
- reconstruct an argument
- a reconstructed account

### Example
> Historians often reconstruct a sequence of events from incomplete records rather than from one perfect source.

### Word family
- **reconstruction** (n.)
- **reconstructive** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **recall** = 从记忆中想起
- **reconstruct** = 利用多个片段重新组成一个整体，可能涉及推断

## 6. misidentify

**Meaning:** 误认；把一个人、物体、原因或类别错误识别成另一个。

### Collocations
- misidentify a person
- misidentify the source
- misidentify the cause
- be misidentified as...
- eyewitness misidentification

### Example
> If two samples are labeled poorly, a researcher may misidentify which condition produced a particular result.

### Word family
- **misidentification** (n.)
- **identify** (v.)
- **identification** (n.)

### Contrast
- **misunderstand** = 理解错意思
- **misidentify** = 认错“是谁 / 是什么 / 属于哪一类”

The prefix **mis-** reliably means “wrongly” in words such as **misread, misjudge, misinterpret, misclassify**.

## 7. overstate

**Meaning:** 夸大；把证据、效果、差异或结论说得比实际支持程度更强。

### Collocations
- overstate the evidence
- overstate the effect
- overstate the importance of...
- be easy to overstate
- the claim is overstated

### Example
> It would overstate the evidence to describe a small short-term improvement as proof of a permanent benefit.

### Word family
- **overstatement** (n.)
- **overstated** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **exaggerate** = 更普通，可用于日常夸张
- **overstate** = 在分析、新闻、政策和学术写作中很常见，强调“表述强度超过证据”

## 8. generalize from A to B

**Meaning:** 从 A 推广到 B；把在一个样本、任务、环境或群体中得到的结论延伸到更广泛对象。

### Collocations
- generalize from a sample to a population
- generalize from one task to another
- generalize across settings
- difficult to generalize
- the findings may not generalize to...

### Example
> Results from a small group of volunteers may not generalize to the entire population.

### Word family
- **generalize** (v.)
- **generalization** (n.)
- **generalizable** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **apply** = 把方法、规则或发现用于某处
- **generalize** = 假设原有结论在更广对象或条件中仍成立

## Reusable mini-patterns

### Memory
> A strong **retrieval cue** can make stored information easier to access.

### Persistence
> Repetition alone does not guarantee that learners will **retain** information over time.

### Functional effect
> Sleep loss can **impair** performance even when a person feels capable of continuing.

### Attention
> Repeated **distractions** can increase the cost of returning to a demanding task.

### Reconstruction
> People may **reconstruct** an event from partial information rather than replaying it exactly.

### Identification
> Poor evidence can lead observers to **misidentify** the source of a problem.

### Evidence strength
> It would **overstate the evidence** to treat an association as proof of causation.

### Scope
> Researchers should be cautious when **generalizing from** one narrow task **to** everyday behavior.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise option and explain why.

1. A notification can serve as a **retrieval cue / storage box** for an intended task.
2. The study tested whether participants could **retain / rent** information after a delay.
3. Continuous noise may **impair / erase completely** concentration without making work impossible.
4. A passing conversation can become a **distraction / conclusion** during difficult reading.
5. Witnesses may **reconstruct / photocopy** an event using incomplete memory fragments.
6. A person can **misidentify / misunderstand grammatically** a stranger as someone they have seen before.
7. Calling one experiment “proof that phones destroy memory” would **overstate / understate** the evidence.
8. Results from a highly controlled task may not **generalize to / memorize from** everyday behavior.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. **retrieval cue** — it triggers access to information already stored.
> 2. **retain** — the question concerns whether information remains available after time has passed.
> 3. **impair** — performance can become worse without being completely eliminated.
> 4. **distraction** — it pulls attention away from the main task.
> 5. **reconstruct** — the event is rebuilt from fragments rather than copied exactly.
> 6. **misidentify** — the error concerns who the person is.
> 7. **overstate** — the claim is stronger and broader than the experiment supports.
> 8. **generalize to** — the issue is whether a result extends beyond the original setting.

## Retrieval quiz

Try without looking back.

1. What is a **retrieval cue**?
2. Complete: Students may ______ more information if they test themselves after a delay.
3. Which verb means “reduce the quality of a function or ability without necessarily eliminating it”?
4. What is the difference between an **interruption** and a **distraction**?
5. Complete: Historians sometimes ______ events from incomplete evidence.
6. Which verb means to identify a person, object, or cause incorrectly?
7. Complete: The headline may ______ the strength of the evidence.
8. What does it mean to **generalize from a sample to a population**?
9. Why is **retain** more precise than **remember** in some learning studies?
10. Rewrite this claim more cautiously: *This one experiment proves that digital tools damage memory.*

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. A clue or feature of the context that helps stored information become accessible.
> 2. retain
> 3. impair
> 4. An interruption stops or breaks an ongoing activity; a distraction draws attention away and may or may not fully interrupt the activity.
> 5. reconstruct
> 6. misidentify
> 7. overstate
> 8. To assume that a result observed in a sample also applies to the wider population.
> 9. **Retain** emphasizes whether information remains available over time, while **remember** is broader and more general.
> 10. One experiment may show a task-specific effect of a digital tool on memory performance, but it does not by itself establish a general decline in memory.
