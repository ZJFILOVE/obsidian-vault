# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-22

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-22|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-22|Today's translation]]

Today's set partly connects to the FTC reading, but the goal is broader: reusable vocabulary for **information visibility, choice architecture, consent, evaluation, and cautious criticism**. Focus on collocations and contrasts rather than isolated Chinese equivalents.

## 1. disclose

**Meaning:** 披露；公开原本未明确提供的信息，尤其常用于价格、风险、利益关系、条款、数据使用等较正式语境。

### Collocations
- disclose information
- disclose a fee
- disclose a conflict of interest
- disclose personal data
- full disclosure
- disclosure requirements

### Example
> Companies should disclose additional fees before customers reach the final stage of payment.

### Contrast
- **tell** = 最普通的“告诉”
- **reveal** = 揭示原本未知或隐藏的东西，可用于新闻、秘密、结果
- **disclose** = 更正式，常涉及法律、商业、研究伦理和信息义务

### Word family
- **disclosure** (n.) — 披露；公开的信息
- **non-disclosure** (n./adj.) — 不披露；保密相关

## 2. obscure

**Meaning:** 使难以看清、理解或注意；也可作形容词表示“晦涩的、不知名的”。今天重点掌握动词用法。

### Collocations
- obscure important information
- obscure the true cost
- obscure a distinction
- be obscured by...
- deliberately obscure

### Example
> A complicated pricing table can obscure the total cost even when each individual charge is technically listed.

### Contrast
- **hide** = 直接隐藏，语义较强
- **obscure** = 使信息难以看清、理解或注意，不一定完全不可见

This distinction is useful in academic writing because information can be **available but obscured**.

## 3. steer

**Meaning:** 原义是“操纵方向”；引申为引导某人朝某个选择、结果或方向行动。

### Collocations
- steer users toward an option
- steer a discussion toward...
- steer investment toward...
- steer someone away from...
- subtly steer behavior

### Example
> The order in which options are presented can steer users toward one choice without removing the alternatives.

### Contrast
- **force** = 强迫，几乎不给选择
- **persuade** = 通过理由或劝说改变想法
- **steer** = 通过环境、设计或引导影响选择方向，通常仍保留形式上的选择

## 4. default

**Meaning:** 默认设置；在用户不主动改变时系统自动采用的选项。作形容词可表示“默认的”。

### Collocations
- default setting
- default option
- be selected by default
- change the default
- default configuration

### Example
> A privacy-friendly default can protect users who never open the settings menu.

### Contrast
A **default** is not necessarily a recommendation. It is simply the option that applies automatically unless the user changes it.

## 5. consent

**Meaning:** 同意；在医疗、研究、法律、隐私和数据处理中常强调知情、自愿、明确的同意。

### Collocations
- obtain consent
- informed consent
- explicit consent
- consent to data collection
- without consent

### Example
> Meaningful consent requires more than placing a difficult-to-read notice at the bottom of a page.

### Contrast
- **agreement** is broad and can refer to opinions, contracts, or arrangements.
- **consent** often emphasizes permission given by a person who has a genuine choice.

### Grammar note
Use **consent to + noun / -ing**:
- consent to the procedure
- consent to sharing data

## 6. misleading

**Meaning:** 误导性的；使人形成错误印象，即使某句话本身不一定完全虚假。

### Collocations
- misleading claim
- misleading impression
- potentially misleading
- materially misleading
- misleading advertising

### Example
> Reporting only the percentage increase can be misleading if the starting value was extremely small.

### Contrast
- **false** = 直接不真实
- **misleading** = 可能包含真实元素，但整体呈现方式容易让人得出错误结论

This is a very useful distinction in evidence-based writing.

## 7. opt out

**Meaning:** 主动选择退出某项服务、数据收集、默认安排或计划。

### Collocations
- opt out of data collection
- opt out of a program
- allow users to opt out
- an opt-out mechanism
- easy to opt out

### Example
> Users are more likely to exercise a choice when the option to opt out is easy to find and understand.

### Contrast
- **opt in** = 主动选择加入
- **opt out** = 默认可能已加入，但用户可以主动退出

### Grammar note
Use **opt out of + noun / -ing**.

## 8. scrutiny

**Meaning:** 仔细审查；严格检查。常用于政策、研究、公司行为、公共机构和证据评价。

### Collocations
- come under scrutiny
- face public scrutiny
- withstand scrutiny
- close scrutiny
- regulatory scrutiny

### Example
> Claims based on a small or unusual sample may not withstand close scrutiny.

### Contrast
- **attention** = 注意，范围很广
- **inspection** = 实地或程序性的检查
- **scrutiny** = 细致、批判性地审查，常带有“经得起质疑吗”的意味

## 9. comply with

**Meaning:** 遵守规则、法律、要求、标准或正式请求。

### Collocations
- comply with the law
- comply with regulations
- comply with a requirement
- comply with safety standards
- ensure compliance with...

### Example
> A company may change its interface to comply with new privacy requirements.

### Word family
- **compliance** (n.) — 遵守；合规
- **compliant** (adj.) — 符合要求的；合规的
- **non-compliance** (n.) — 不合规

### Contrast
**follow** is general. **Comply with** is especially common when the requirement is formal, regulatory, contractual, or institutional.

## Reusable mini-patterns

### Information visibility
> A notice may technically **disclose** a fee while still **obscuring** how much the customer will pay in total.

### Choice architecture
> A **default option** can **steer** behavior even when users remain free to choose another option.

### Permission and control
> Users should be able to give meaningful **consent** and easily **opt out of** optional data collection.

### Careful criticism
> A statement can be **misleading** without being literally false.

### Regulation and evidence
> Practices that come under regulatory **scrutiny** may be changed to **comply with** new requirements.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise word or phrase and explain why.

1. The company **disclosed / told** the additional fee in its formal terms and conditions.
2. The information was not completely hidden, but the layout **obscured / deleted** its importance.
3. The interface **steered / forced** users toward the annual plan, although a monthly plan was still available.
4. The box was already checked because it was the **default / compulsory** option.
5. The statement used accurate numbers but created a **misleading / false** impression about the size of the effect.
6. Users could **opt out of / resign from** promotional emails at any time.
7. The claim came under close **scrutiny / eyesight** after researchers questioned the sample.
8. The company changed the process to **comply with / obey to** the regulation.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. **disclosed** — formal information-provision context
> 2. **obscured** — the information remained present but difficult to notice or interpret
> 3. **steered** — alternatives still existed, so “forced” is too strong
> 4. **default** — automatically selected unless changed
> 5. **misleading** — the numbers may be true even though the overall impression is distorted
> 6. **opt out of** — standard expression for withdrawing from an optional service or setting
> 7. **scrutiny** — careful critical examination
> 8. **comply with** — the correct collocation is **comply with**, not *comply to*

## Retrieval quiz

Try without looking back.

1. Which verb is commonly used for formally making fees, risks, or conflicts of interest known?
2. Complete: A complicated interface may ______ the total cost without completely hiding it.
3. What is the difference between **steer** and **force**?
4. What is a **default option**?
5. Complete: Researchers must obtain informed ______ before enrolling participants in many kinds of studies.
6. Why can a statement be **misleading** even if every individual number in it is technically correct?
7. Complete: Users should be able to ______ out of non-essential data collection.
8. What does it mean for a claim to **withstand scrutiny**?
9. Complete: The company changed its policy to ______ with the new regulation.
10. Give one situation in which **disclose** is more natural than **tell**.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. disclose
> 2. obscure
> 3. **Steer** means influence the direction of a choice while alternatives may remain; **force** implies that meaningful choice is removed or severely constrained.
> 4. The option or setting that applies automatically unless the user actively changes it.
> 5. consent
> 6. Because selection, framing, omitted context, or comparison choices can create an incorrect overall impression even when the raw figures are accurate.
> 7. opt
> 8. It survives careful critical examination without its main claim collapsing.
> 9. comply
> 10. Example: A researcher must **disclose a conflict of interest** in a publication.
