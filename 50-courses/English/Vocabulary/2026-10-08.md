# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-08

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-08|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-08|Today's translation]]

The aim is **transferable language**, not a glossary of oceanography. Some chunks connect to the NOAA source; others help with reports, institutional decisions, and careful arguments. Learn the collocation and the precision contrast, not only the Chinese gloss.

## 1. disperse / be dispersed

**Meaning:** 分散；散布到较广区域。Often describes particles, people, resources, or information moving apart.

**Collocations**
- disperse over a wide area
- be widely dispersed
- disperse a crowd
- dispersed throughout the region
- spatial dispersion (noun)

**Word family:** disperse (v.); dispersion (n.); dispersed (adj./past participle).

**Example:** Small businesses are dispersed across several neighborhoods, making a single centralized pickup point inconvenient.

**Contrast:** **disperse** = spread apart; **dissolve** = a substance goes into solution (or an organization formally ends). A plastic fragment can be dispersed without dissolving.

## 2. accumulate / build up

**Meaning:** 逐渐积累；堆积（通常暗含时间过程）。

**Collocations**
- accumulate over time
- accumulate in one area
- accumulate evidence / wealth / debt
- a buildup of pressure
- gradual accumulation

**Word family:** accumulation (n.); cumulative (adj., concerning a total over time). **Cumulative** is not always interchangeable with **accumulated**.

**Example:** A backlog of unanswered requests can build up even when staff respond quickly to simple cases.

**Contrast:** **accumulate** highlights growth in quantity over time; **concentrate** highlights a higher density or location. Debris may both accumulate and be locally concentrated.

## 3. trace something back to

**Meaning:** 追溯到某个来源、事件或原因。

**Collocations**
- trace an error back to a configuration change
- trace the source of a claim
- be difficult to trace back to
- trace the problem to an earlier decision

**Example:** The team traced several missing records back to a software update, but other discrepancies remained unexplained.

**Contrast:** **trace to** describes a supported provenance chain; **attribute to** assigns causal responsibility. Finding two things together does not mean one can be confidently attributed to the other.

## 4. pose a risk / threat to

**Meaning:** 对……构成风险、威胁（不等于一定造成损害）。

**Collocations**
- pose a risk to public safety
- pose a threat to wildlife
- pose a serious / potential hazard
- pose challenges for policymakers

**Example:** An unclear evacuation sign may pose a risk to visitors in an emergency.

**Contrast:** **pose a risk** is prospective; **cause harm** asserts a harmful outcome. Use the weaker wording when the outcome is possible rather than observed.

## 5. be entangled in

**Meaning:** 被缠住；也可引申为卷入复杂关系或争端。

**Collocations**
- become entangled in a fishing net
- be entangled in legal disputes
- disentangle two effects
- get caught in a complicated process

**Word family:** entangle (v.); entanglement (n.); disentangle (v.).

**Example:** The project became entangled in a dispute over who was responsible for maintaining the shared equipment.

**Contrast:** Literal **entanglement** is physical trapping; figurative **entanglement** is involvement in a difficult situation. **Disentangle the effects** in academic writing means analytically separate influences, not physically untie something.

## 6. prevent something from doing something

**Meaning:** 阻止某事发生；阻止某物进入某一过程。

**Collocations**
- prevent accidents from occurring
- prevent data from being lost
- prevent debris from entering waterways
- prevention is preferable to remediation (in appropriate contexts)

**Example:** Automatic backups can prevent a temporary device failure from causing permanent data loss.

**Grammar:** **prevent + object + from + -ing**. Avoid **prevent to do**. **Avoid** may take a noun or -ing form without the same structure: **avoid losing data**.

## 7. address the root cause

**Meaning:** 处理根本原因，而不仅是可见症状。

**Collocations**
- address the underlying problem
- identify the root cause
- treat the symptoms
- root-cause analysis
- tackle a structural issue

**Example:** Adding more customer-support staff may shorten waiting times, but it will not address the root cause if the application keeps failing.

**Contrast:** **Mitigate** reduces severity; **remedy** corrects a problem; **prevent** stops occurrence; **address the root cause** targets the generating mechanism. Sometimes several responses are needed.

## 8. take something into account

**Meaning:** 将某项因素纳入判断、计算或计划。

**Collocations**
- take local conditions into account
- take costs into account
- take account of differences between groups
- fail to take uncertainty into account

**Example:** The committee should take transport time into account when comparing the accessibility of different venues.

**Contrast:** **consider** is a broad verb. **Take into account** highlights incorporating a relevant variable in a decision or estimate. Never say **take into the account** in this sense.

## 9. fall short of

**Meaning:** 未达到目标、标准或期望；差一点或差得较多都可以。

**Collocations**
- fall short of expectations
- fall short of the target
- fall well short of the required standard
- fall short by a narrow margin

**Example:** The pilot program attracted many sign-ups but fell short of its participation target after accounting for no-shows.

**Contrast:** **Fail** can be categorical; **fall short of** explicitly compares the outcome with a stated benchmark. **Be short of** often means lack a resource: **We are short of staff.**

## Active recall — one-minute contrast drill

Explain the difference without looking back:

| Pair | Distinction to retrieve |
| --- | --- |
| disperse / dissolve | location versus physical or chemical state |
| accumulate / concentrate | growth over time versus density |
| trace to / attribute to | documented origin versus assigned cause |
| pose a risk / cause harm | potential versus observed outcome |
| mitigate / prevent | reduce severity versus stop occurrence |
| fall short of / be short of | below a benchmark versus lacking a resource |

> [!answer]- Contrast check
> **Disperse** is spread in space; **dissolve** is incorporation in solution. **Accumulate** stresses growth in amount; **concentrate** stresses density. **Trace to** can establish provenance; **attribute to** adds a causal claim. **Pose a risk** does not assert harm occurred; **cause harm** does. **Mitigate** reduces an impact; **prevent** aims to keep it from occurring. **Fall short of** is a benchmark comparison; **be short of** commonly denotes insufficient supply.
