# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-28

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-28|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-28|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary partly connects to the earthquake early-warning article, but the target is broader: reusable English for discussing **systems, delays, constraints, feasibility, decisions, and realistic performance**.

## 1. latency

**Meaning:** 延迟；从输入、请求或事件发生到系统响应之间的时间差。常用于网络、计算、通信和控制系统。

### Collocations
- network latency
- communication latency
- low-latency system
- reduce latency
- latency-sensitive application

### Example
> Even a small amount of network latency can matter when a system must respond within seconds.

### Contrast
- **delay** = 最广泛的“延迟”
- **latency** = 更技术化，强调系统中可测量的响应时间

## 2. constraint

**Meaning:** 约束；限制条件。强调“必须在这个条件下工作”。

### Collocations
- physical constraint
- technical constraint
- budget constraint
- time constraint
- operate under constraints
- impose a constraint on

### Example
> The team improved the algorithm, but battery capacity remained a hard constraint on the device.

### Word family
- **constrain** (v.)
- **constrained** (adj.)

### Contrast
A **problem** may be solved; a **constraint** may have to be accepted, reduced, or designed around.

## 3. bottleneck

**Meaning:** 瓶颈；限制整个流程速度或容量的最慢环节。

### Collocations
- a major bottleneck
- identify the bottleneck
- remove / relieve a bottleneck
- performance bottleneck
- become the bottleneck

### Example
> Adding more servers did not help because database access, not computing power, was the real bottleneck.

### Contrast
**Weakness** is general; **bottleneck** specifically limits the throughput or speed of the whole process.

## 4. feasible

**Meaning:** 可行的；在现实条件下能够实施或完成的。

### Collocations
- technically feasible
- economically feasible
- financially feasible
- a feasible option
- a feasible solution

### Example
> The proposal is technically feasible, but it may be too expensive to deploy at scale.

### Word family
- **feasibility** (n.)
- **infeasible** (adj.)

### Contrast
**Possible** may mean possible in principle. **Feasible** means practical under real constraints.

## 5. actionable

**Meaning:** 可据此采取行动的；信息足够及时、具体、可靠，使接收者能够真正做出下一步决定。

### Collocations
- actionable information
- actionable warning
- actionable insight
- actionable recommendation

### Example
> A weather update is useful only if it arrives early enough to be actionable.

### Contrast
**Informative** means useful to know; **actionable** means usable for a concrete decision or action.

## 6. mitigate

**Meaning:** 缓解；减轻不良影响、风险或严重程度，通常不表示彻底消除。

### Collocations
- mitigate risk
- mitigate damage
- mitigate the impact of
- mitigation measures
- risk mitigation

### Example
> Better ventilation can mitigate some indoor-air risks, although it does not remove every source of exposure.

### Word family
- **mitigation** (n.)

### Contrast
- **eliminate** = 消除
- **prevent** = 防止发生
- **mitigate** = 降低严重程度

## 7. account for

**Meaning 1:** 把……考虑进去。

> The model should account for seasonal changes in demand.

**Meaning 2:** 解释；构成某结果的原因。

> A small number of products account for most of the sales.

### Collocations
- account for uncertainty
- account for variation
- account for additional delay
- account for a large proportion of

### Contrast
- **account for X in the model** = 把 X 考虑进去
- **X accounts for the difference** = X 解释了这个差异

## 8. fall short of

**Meaning:** 未达到某个标准、目标、承诺或预期。

### Collocations
- fall short of expectations
- fall short of the target
- fall short of the required standard
- fall well short of

### Example
> The prototype works in controlled tests but still falls short of the reliability required for clinical use.

### Contrast
**Fail** may imply complete failure. **Fall short of** means some progress exists, but the target has not been met.

## 9. align expectations with

**Meaning:** 让预期与现实能力、证据或条件保持一致。

### Collocations
- align expectations with reality
- align expectations with available evidence
- align goals with resources
- align incentives with outcomes

### Example
> Clear communication helps align users' expectations with what the system can actually deliver.

### Word family
- **align** (v.)
- **alignment** (n.)
- **misalignment** (n.)

### Contrast
**Lower expectations** only means reducing them. **Align expectations with reality** means calibrating them accurately.

## Reusable mini-patterns

> The main **bottleneck** is communication **latency**, not computation.

> A solution may be theoretically possible but not economically **feasible**.

> Information must arrive in time to be **actionable**.

> The intervention cannot eliminate the hazard, but it may **mitigate** its consequences.

> Any realistic estimate should **account for** user response time.

> The system performs well in some settings but **falls short of** its advertised target in others.

> Public explanations should **align expectations with** what the technology can reliably deliver.
