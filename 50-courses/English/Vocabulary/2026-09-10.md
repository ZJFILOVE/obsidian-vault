## 1. hypothesis /haɪˈpɑːθəsɪs/

**n. 假说；有待检验的解释**

### Useful language
- formulate a hypothesis
- test a hypothesis
- support / reject a hypothesis

### Memory
From Greek elements meaning roughly **“putting/supposing under”**: *hypo-* (under) + *thesis* (placing/proposition). Think of a hypothesis as a proposition placed underneath an investigation and then tested.

### Example
> A plausible hypothesis still needs to be tested against evidence.

## 2. plausible /ˈplɔːzəbəl/

**adj. 看似合理的；有可能成立的**

- a plausible explanation
- a plausible mechanism
- sound plausible

Do not confuse **plausible** with **proven**: an idea can be plausible without being established.

> Several plausible explanations can fit the same observation.

## 3. definitive /dɪˈfɪnətɪv/

**adj. 最终的；权威性的；明确解决问题的**

- a definitive answer
- definitive evidence
- a definitive study

### Word family
- define
- definition
- definitive

The family ultimately relates to setting a boundary or limit; a **definitive** answer settles the boundaries of a question rather than merely suggesting a possibility.

> Researchers still lack a definitive explanation for many features of dreaming.

## 4. consolidate /kənˈsɑːlədeɪt/

**v. 巩固；合并；使更牢固**

- consolidate a memory
- consolidate knowledge
- consolidate several departments

### Memory
The Latin root is related to **solid**. To consolidate something is, metaphorically, to make it more solid.

> Sleep may help the brain consolidate newly acquired memories.

## 5. interpret /ɪnˈtɜːrprət/

**v. 解释；解读；理解……的意义**

- interpret the results
- interpret evidence cautiously
- interpret a dream

### Word family
- interpretation
- interpreter
- misinterpret

> The same result can be interpreted differently under competing models.

## 6. account for

**chunk. 解释；说明……的原因；占（比例）**

- account for an observation
- account for the difference
- account for 30 percent of...

> No single theory currently accounts for every feature of dreaming.

In academic English, **account for** is often more precise than repeatedly using *explain*.

## 7. compelling /kəmˈpelɪŋ/

**adj. 有说服力的；引人注目的；难以抗拒的**

- compelling evidence
- a compelling argument
- a compelling reason

### Word family
- compel (v.)
- compelling (adj.)

> A vivid example can be compelling, but it is not necessarily strong evidence.

## 8. tentative /ˈtentətɪv/

**adj. 暂定的；试探性的；尚未确定的**

- a tentative conclusion
- a tentative explanation
- a tentative agreement

### Memory
Historically related to the idea of **trying/testing**. A tentative conclusion is one you are willing to revise when better evidence appears.

> The researchers offered a tentative explanation rather than making a sweeping claim.
