# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-18

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-18|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-18|Today's translation]]

Today's set focuses on **heritage, context, interpretation, and careful comparison**. Several items connect to the Met conversation, but each is chosen because it also works in academic writing, news, travel, history, policy, or ordinary explanation.

## 1. heritage

**Meaning:** 遗产；由过去传承下来的文化、传统、建筑、语言、知识或身份资源

### Collocations
- cultural heritage
- architectural heritage
- shared heritage
- preserve / protect heritage
- heritage site
- intangible cultural heritage

### Example
> Restoring a historic building can preserve part of a city's architectural heritage, even if the building's original use has changed.

### Contrast
- **history** = what happened in the past, or the study of it
- **heritage** = what people inherit, value, preserve, or identify with from the past

A historical object is not automatically meaningful as heritage to every group in the same way.

## 2. preserve

**Meaning:** 保存；保护某物使其免于损坏、消失或重大改变

### Collocations
- preserve evidence
- preserve a building
- preserve biodiversity
- preserve cultural traditions
- preserve the original structure
- well-preserved remains

### Example
> Digitization can preserve access to fragile documents even when the originals cannot be handled frequently.

### Contrast: preserve vs. conserve
Both can mean “保护、保存.” **Preserve** is broader and often emphasizes keeping something from loss or change. **Conserve** often emphasizes careful management of limited resources or professional care of objects, ecosystems, or materials.

## 3. displace / displacement

**Meaning:** 使离开原来的位置、家园或环境；取代。**displacement** 表示这种移动、迁移或替代本身。

### Collocations
- displaced people
- forced displacement
- displace a community
- be displaced by development
- technological displacement

### Example
> Large infrastructure projects can create economic benefits while also displacing communities from long-established neighborhoods.

### Usage note
**Displace** can describe both people and things. In technology discussions, automation may **displace workers** or **displace an older technology**. Context determines whether the meaning is physical movement, social disruption, or replacement.

## 4. reconstruct

**Meaning:** 重建；根据剩余证据、记录或模型重新建立某物，也可指重新推断过去发生了什么

### Collocations
- reconstruct a building
- reconstruct an event
- reconstruct the past
- digitally reconstruct
- a reconstructed version

### Example
> Researchers used photographs and surviving plans to reconstruct the layout of the original site.

### Word formation
**re-** means “again,” and **construct** means “build.” This is a reliable and useful morphological clue: **reconstruct = build again / infer again from evidence**.

### Contrast
- **restore** = repair something so that it more closely resembles an earlier condition
- **reconstruct** = rebuild something that may be partly or largely missing

## 5. constitute

**Meaning:** 构成；形成某个整体的重要部分；在较正式语境中也可表示“算作、构成某种情况”

### Collocations
- constitute a major part of...
- constitute evidence
- constitute a threat
- jointly constitute...
- be constituted by...

### Example
> Access to a shared language can constitute an important part of community identity without being the only factor that defines it.

### Contrast
**make up** is more conversational. **Constitute** is more formal and useful in academic, legal, and analytical writing.

## 6. juxtapose / juxtaposition

**Meaning:** 把两个事物并置，以突出它们之间的相似、差异或张力；**juxtaposition** 是“并置、对照”

### Collocations
- juxtapose A with B
- a striking juxtaposition
- the juxtaposition of old and new
- create a visual juxtaposition

### Example
> The exhibition juxtaposes ordinary household objects with luxury goods to highlight differences in social status.

### Usage note
A **comparison** can be explicit and analytical. A **juxtaposition** often means that the two things are placed next to each other so that the contrast or relationship becomes noticeable without a long explanation.

## 7. evoke

**Meaning:** 唤起、引起某种记忆、情绪、形象或联想

### Collocations
- evoke a memory
- evoke a sense of...
- evoke strong emotions
- evoke the atmosphere of...
- vividly evoke

### Example
> The restored station is designed to evoke the atmosphere of early twentieth-century rail travel.

### Contrast
- **invoke** = formally call on, cite, or appeal to something, such as a law, principle, or authority
- **evoke** = bring a feeling, image, or memory to mind

A common mistake is to swap these two because they look similar.

## 8. disregard

**Meaning:** 忽视；不考虑某个本应被考虑的事实、风险、规则或利益

### Collocations
- disregard evidence
- disregard a warning
- disregard the needs of...
- show disregard for...
- cannot simply disregard...

### Example
> A policy that improves average efficiency may still be unfair if it disregards the needs of people with limited access to technology.

### Contrast
**ignore** is very common and broad. **Disregard** is more formal and often suggests that something relevant has been consciously or carelessly left out of consideration.

## 9. come into contact with

**Meaning:** 接触到；与人、物、文化、信息或物质发生实际接触

### Collocations
- come into contact with a culture
- come into contact with new ideas
- come into contact with a chemical
- bring A into contact with B

### Example
> Travel can bring students into contact with unfamiliar social norms that challenge their assumptions.

### Contrast
**learn about** means gaining information. **Come into contact with** emphasizes direct exposure or encounter.

## Useful mini-patterns

### Preservation with a qualification
> The project **preserves** important physical evidence, but it does not necessarily preserve the object's original social context.

### Context and interpretation
> Once an object is **displaced** from its original setting, viewers may interpret it differently.

### Comparison through placement
> The exhibition **juxtaposes** two periods to make the change in social values more visible.

### Cautious identity claim
> Language may **constitute** an important part of cultural identity without defining that identity completely.

### Evidence-based reconstruction
> Historians **reconstruct** past events from incomplete records, so some uncertainty usually remains.
