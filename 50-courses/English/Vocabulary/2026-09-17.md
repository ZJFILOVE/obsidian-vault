# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-17

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-17|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-17|Today's translation]]

Today's set focuses on language for **shared stories, expectations, evidence, and feedback effects**. Several items connect to the IMF article, but the goal is reusable English for economics, social science, research writing, and ordinary argument.

## 1. narrative

**Meaning:** 叙事；用来组织、解释某件事的一套故事或解释框架

### Collocations
- dominant narrative
- public narrative
- economic narrative
- challenge a narrative
- shape a narrative
- construct a narrative around...

### Example
> A simple narrative can influence how people interpret the same set of economic statistics.

### Contrast
- **story** is broad and everyday.
- **narrative** often emphasizes the way events are framed and connected into a meaningful explanation.

A narrative does not have to be false. It can be accurate, misleading, incomplete, or simply one of several possible interpretations.

## 2. sentiment

**Meaning:** 情绪倾向；一群人整体表现出的乐观、悲观、信心或态度

### Collocations
- consumer sentiment
- investor sentiment
- market sentiment
- public sentiment
- a shift in sentiment

### Example
> Consumer sentiment improved before household spending showed a clear recovery.

### Contrast
**emotion** usually refers to a specific feeling experienced by a person. **Sentiment** is especially useful when talking about a broader or aggregated attitude among many people.

## 3. fundamentals

**Meaning:** 基本面；决定一个企业、市场或系统长期表现的基础因素

### Collocations
- economic fundamentals
- strong / weak fundamentals
- company fundamentals
- underlying fundamentals

### Example
> Short-term optimism can push expectations upward even when the company's underlying fundamentals have changed very little.

### Usage note
In finance and economics, **fundamentals** often refers to measurable factors such as earnings, productivity, debt, demand, or growth rather than mood or speculation.

## 4. bear the hallmarks of...

**Meaning:** 具有……的典型特征；显示出……的明显迹象

### Collocations
- bear the hallmarks of careful planning
- bear the hallmarks of a speculative bubble
- bear the hallmarks of systematic bias
- bear the hallmarks of a coordinated campaign

### Example
> The pattern bears the hallmarks of a feedback process rather than a one-time reaction.

### Register
This is more formal than **look like** and is especially useful when several features collectively point toward an interpretation.

## 5. reinforce

**Meaning:** 加强；强化已经存在的趋势、信念、结构或行为

### Collocations
- reinforce a belief
- reinforce an existing trend
- reinforce inequality
- mutually reinforcing effects
- reinforce one another

### Example
> Rising demand can reinforce optimistic expectations, which may encourage further investment.

### Contrast
- **cause** = produce an effect directly
- **reinforce** = make something that already exists stronger

This distinction is useful when you want to avoid overstating causality.

## 6. quantify

**Meaning:** 量化；把某个概念转化为可以用数字衡量或比较的形式

### Collocations
- quantify an effect
- quantify uncertainty
- difficult to quantify
- quantify the contribution of...

### Word family
- **quantity** (n.)
- **quantitative** (adj.)
- **quantify** (v.)
- **quantification** (n.)

### Example
> Researchers developed a method to quantify how frequently certain themes appeared in corporate communication.

### Contrast
**measure** is broader. To **quantify** something specifically means expressing its magnitude numerically.

## 7. aggregate

**Meaning:** 汇总的；总体的 / 汇总、合计

### Collocations
- aggregate demand
- aggregate data
- aggregate level
- aggregate effect
- aggregate several measures

### Example
> A stable aggregate trend can hide substantial variation across individual firms.

### Contrast
- **individual-level** = 单个个体或单位层面
- **aggregate-level** = 将很多个体合并后的总体层面

Do not assume that a relationship found at the aggregate level must also hold for every individual case.

## 8. account for

**Meaning:** 解释；占据；构成……的一部分原因或比例

### Collocations
- account for the difference
- account for variation in...
- account for a large share of...
- fail to account for...

### Example
> Differences in prior experience may account for part of the performance gap between the two groups.

### Contrast
**account for** often expresses partial explanation. It is usually weaker and more precise than saying one factor **caused** the entire outcome.

## 9. self-fulfilling

**Meaning:** 自我实现的；某种预期因为影响了人的行为，最终使结果更可能真的发生

### Collocations
- self-fulfilling prophecy
- self-fulfilling expectation
- self-fulfilling cycle

### Example
> If enough consumers expect a shortage and start buying unusually large quantities, the expectation can become partly self-fulfilling.

### Memory note
The logic matters more than the label: **belief → behavior → changed outcome → belief appears confirmed**.

## Useful mini-patterns

### Cautious causal claim
> X may **contribute to** Y, but it does not necessarily **account for** the entire effect.

### Feedback loop
> A belief can alter behavior, and the resulting behavior can in turn **reinforce** the original belief.

### Evidence and interpretation
> The data **bear the hallmarks of** a broader pattern, but further evidence is needed to establish the mechanism.

### Individual vs. overall trend
> The relationship is visible **at the aggregate level**, although individual cases vary substantially.
