# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-24

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-24|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-24|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary partly connects to the linguistics video, but the target is broader: reusable language for **classification, standards, overlap, boundaries, and social or historical influence**. Focus on collocations and contrasts rather than memorizing isolated Chinese equivalents.

## 1. distinction

**Meaning:** 区别；区分。常用于说明两个概念之间存在有意义的差别。

### Collocations
- draw a distinction between A and B
- make a distinction between...
- an important distinction
- a useful distinction
- a clear / sharp distinction
- the distinction becomes blurred

### Example
> A useful distinction can be drawn between measuring how often people interact and measuring whether those interactions are productive.

### Word family
- **distinguish** (v.)
- **distinct** (adj.)
- **distinctive** (adj.)
- **indistinguishable** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **difference** = 最普通的“不同”
- **distinction** = 更强调分析、分类或概念层面的“区分”

Academic writing often uses **distinction** when the writer is defining categories rather than merely noticing that two things differ.

## 2. criterion / criteria

**Meaning:** 标准；判据。**Criterion** 是单数，**criteria** 是复数。

### Collocations
- a selection criterion
- a useful criterion
- meet a criterion
- satisfy the criteria
- apply the same criteria
- use X as a criterion for...

### Example
> Mutual intelligibility may be a useful criterion, but it is not always sufficient on its own.

### Grammar note
- one **criterion**
- several **criteria**

A common learner error is using *criterias*. Avoid it.

### Contrast
- **standard** can mean a level of quality or an established norm.
- **criterion** is a specific basis used to judge, classify, or decide something.

## 3. mutually intelligible

**Meaning:** 相互可理解的；双方各自使用自己的语言或言语形式时，仍能在相当程度上理解彼此。

### Collocations
- be mutually intelligible
- largely / partly mutually intelligible
- a high degree of mutual intelligibility
- lack mutual intelligibility

### Example
> Two speech varieties can be partly mutually intelligible even if they are officially treated as separate languages.

### Word family
- **intelligible** (adj.) — 可理解的
- **intelligibility** (n.) — 可理解度
- **unintelligible** (adj.) — 难以理解的

### Contrast
- **understandable** = 日常、宽泛
- **intelligible** = 更正式，常用于语言、声音、文本或信号是否能被理解

This phrase is somewhat topic-specific, but **intelligible / unintelligible** is broadly useful in academic and technical English.

## 4. continuum

**Meaning:** 连续体；从一个极端逐渐过渡到另一个极端，中间没有天然、清晰断点的范围。

### Collocations
- exist on a continuum
- a broad continuum
- a continuum from A to B
- view something as a continuum
- fall somewhere along a continuum

### Example
> Skill levels are often better understood as a continuum than as two perfectly separate categories.

### Contrast
- **binary** = 二元分类，只有两类
- **continuum** = 连续变化，中间存在大量过渡状态

This is useful in psychology, education, social science, medicine, and any discussion of gradual change.

## 5. standardize

**Meaning:** 使标准化；让程序、格式、语言、测量方法或产品遵循统一规则。

### Collocations
- standardize a procedure
- standardize terminology
- standardize measurement
- a standardized test
- a standardized form
- standardized reporting

### Example
> The laboratory standardized its reporting format so that results from different teams could be compared more easily.

### Word family
- **standard** (n./adj.)
- **standardization** (n.)
- **standardized** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **standardize** = 建立或采用统一形式
- **simplify** = 变简单

A standardized system may still be complicated.

## 6. codify

**Meaning:** 把原本分散或非正式的规则整理成明确、系统、正式的规则体系。常用于法律、语法、技术标准和机构规范。

### Collocations
- codify rules
- codify a practice
- codify standards
- codify existing conventions
- be codified in law

### Example
> Once an informal practice is codified, people may begin to treat it as a formal requirement rather than a flexible convention.

### Word family
- **codification** (n.)

### Contrast
- **write down** = 只是写下来
- **codify** = 系统化并赋予规则结构，通常更正式

### Etymology note
The word is related to **code**, in the sense of an organized body of rules. That connection is genuinely useful: to **codify** is to turn practices into something more like a code.

## 7. convention

**Meaning:** 惯例；约定俗成的做法。并不一定由法律强制，但因为群体长期使用而具有规范作用。

### Collocations
- social convention
- naming convention
- academic convention
- follow a convention
- break with convention
- by convention

### Example
> Citation styles differ across disciplines, but each field develops conventions that make sources easier to identify.

### Contrast
- **rule** = 明确规则，通常更强
- **convention** = 群体约定俗成的做法，可能没有正式强制力

In technical fields, **naming convention** and **coding convention** are especially common.

## 8. draw a boundary between

**Meaning:** 在两个类别、领域或概念之间划定界线。

### Collocations
- draw a boundary between A and B
- draw clear boundaries
- redraw a boundary
- an arbitrary boundary
- a socially constructed boundary

### Example
> It is difficult to draw a precise boundary between advanced beginners and lower-intermediate learners.

### Contrast
- **separate** = 普通动词“分开”
- **draw a boundary between** = 更强调分类者主动决定界线在哪里

This chunk is useful when discussing disciplines, legal categories, age groups, markets, social identities, and scientific classifications.

## 9. blur the line between

**Meaning:** 模糊……之间的界线，使两个类别不再容易清楚分开。

### Collocations
- blur the line between work and leisure
- blur the line between public and private
- blur the line between online and offline life
- increasingly blur the line between...

### Example
> Hybrid courses blur the line between online and classroom learning.

### Contrast
- **erase the line** = 界线几乎完全消失，语义更强
- **blur the line** = 界线仍存在，但变得模糊

This is a very reusable chunk for essays and speaking tasks.

## 10. be shaped by

**Meaning:** 受到……塑造 / 影响。比简单的 **be caused by** 更适合描述长期、多因素形成的结果。

### Collocations
- be shaped by history
- be shaped by social norms
- be shaped by policy
- be shaped by experience
- be shaped by economic conditions
- be shaped by multiple factors

### Example
> Communication habits are shaped by technology, institutional rules, and the expectations of the people using them.

### Contrast
- **be caused by** = 明确因果，通常更强
- **be influenced by** = 一般影响
- **be shaped by** = 强调长期形成过程和多重因素共同作用

This makes **be shaped by** especially useful in history, sociology, culture, education, and policy writing.

## Reusable mini-patterns

### Defining a category
> The most obvious **criterion** is useful, but it does not capture the entire **distinction**.

### Moving away from a binary
> The two categories are better understood as points on a **continuum**.

### Formalizing practice
> Institutions may **standardize** a procedure and later **codify** it in official rules.

### Social norms
> What appears natural may actually reflect a long-standing **convention**.

### Unclear boundaries
> New technology can **blur the line between** two categories that were once easier to separate.

### Historical explanation
> Present-day classifications are often **shaped by** decisions made decades or centuries earlier.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise option and explain why.

1. The admissions committee used academic performance as one **criterion / criterias** for selection.
2. Formality is often a **continuum / switch** rather than a perfect two-category system.
3. The organization **standardized / simplified** its reporting format, even though the new format remained detailed.
4. The rules were later **codified / casually mentioned** in official policy.
5. Academic citation practices are partly a matter of disciplinary **convention / accident**.
6. Remote work can **blur the line between / delete** work time and personal time.
7. It is difficult to **draw a boundary between / pour a boundary between** neighboring categories when the differences are gradual.
8. Educational expectations are often **shaped by / manufactured only by** history and social norms.
9. Two forms of speech may be **mutually intelligible / mutually intelligent** even if they are officially classified separately.
10. Researchers should make a clear **distinction / distance** between an observed association and a causal claim.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. **criterion** — singular after “one”; the plural is **criteria**.
> 2. **continuum** — the change is gradual rather than binary.
> 3. **standardized** — the emphasis is on uniformity, not simplicity.
> 4. **codified** — informal or scattered rules became part of a formal system.
> 5. **convention** — a shared established practice.
> 6. **blur the line between** — the boundary becomes less clear rather than disappearing completely.
> 7. **draw a boundary between** — the normal collocation.
> 8. **shaped by** — multiple long-term influences contribute to the result.
> 9. **mutually intelligible** — **intelligible** means understandable; **intelligent** means smart.
> 10. **distinction** — this is a conceptual difference, not physical distance.
