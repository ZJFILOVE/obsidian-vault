# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-12

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-12|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-12|Today's translation]]

Today's input uses a familiar queue to explain a less familiar idea: the performance of a whole system may be limited by one stage or by interference between stages. Learn these as reusable language for transport, lab work, software, service design, and academic writing—not only for airports.

## 1. bottleneck

**Meaning:** a stage, resource, or condition that limits the performance of an entire process; 瓶颈、制约环节.

**Collocations**
- identify / remove / reduce a bottleneck
- a major bottleneck in production
- create a bottleneck
- the bottleneck stage

**Word family:** *bottleneck* is commonly used as a noun; *bottlenecked* can describe a process whose progress is restricted, though the noun phrase is usually more natural.

**Example:** The instrument's limited availability became a bottleneck in the research workflow.

**Precision contrast:** A **symptom** is what you observe (a long queue); a **bottleneck** is the stage or constraint that limits flow. Do not call every inconvenience a bottleneck unless it restricts the whole process.

## 2. interfere with

**Meaning:** make an activity harder, less effective, or less orderly by obstructing it; 干扰、妨碍.

**Collocations**
- interfere with concentration
- interfere with the normal operation of a device
- interfere with someone's ability to do something
- physical / electromagnetic interference

**Word family:** *interference* (noun), *interfering* (adjective/participle).

**Example:** Repeated interruptions can interfere with careful data analysis.

**Grammar:** Use **interfere with + noun / gerund**. Not *interfere someone's work*.  
**Contrast:** *interrupt* often means stop something temporarily; *interfere with* can describe a broader negative effect even if the activity continues.

## 3. stagger

**Meaning:** arrange events, starts, or arrivals at different times or in a deliberately spaced pattern; 错开、交错安排.

**Collocations**
- stagger arrival times
- stagger the release of information
- a staggered schedule
- staggered shifts

**Word family:** *staggered* (adjective), *staggering* (also means astonishingly large, a different common use).

**Example:** The clinic staggered appointment times to reduce crowding in the waiting room.

**Contrast:** *delay* means make something happen later; *stagger* means arrange multiple events so that they do not all happen at once. Staggering can reduce a peak without reducing the total number of people served.

## 4. throughput

**Meaning:** the amount of work, material, or people a system can process in a given period; 吞吐量、单位时间处理量.

**Collocations**
- increase / measure throughput
- data throughput
- passenger throughput
- throughput per hour

**Related forms:** *throughput* is mainly a technical noun; use *process more items per hour* when writing for a general audience.

**Example:** Adding another checkout station increased throughput during the evening peak.

**Contrast:** **throughput** is what a system actually processes over time; **capacity** is what it can potentially handle under specified conditions. A system may have high capacity but low throughput if a different stage blocks progress.

## 5. streamline

**Meaning:** make a process simpler, smoother, or more efficient, usually by removing unnecessary steps or friction; 精简、优化流程.

**Collocations**
- streamline a procedure / workflow
- streamline approval
- streamline operations
- a streamlined application process

**Word family:** *streamlined* (adjective), *streamlining* (noun/gerund).

**Example:** The department streamlined the reimbursement process but retained the financial checks.

**Contrast:** *simplify* emphasizes making something less complex; *streamline* emphasizes improving flow and efficiency. Streamlining does not automatically mean cutting safeguards or staff.

## 6. be constrained by

**Meaning:** be limited by a condition, rule, resource, or physical boundary; 受到……限制.

**Collocations**
- be constrained by time / budget / space
- resource constraints
- operate under constraints
- a constraint on growth

**Word family:** *constrain* (verb), *constraint* (noun), *constrained* (adjective/participle).

**Example:** The proposed schedule is constrained by the number of rooms available.

**Contrast:** *be caused by* identifies a cause; *be constrained by* identifies a limit on what can be done. A limited budget may constrain a project without being the sole cause of every delay.

## 7. alleviate

**Meaning:** make a problem, burden, or unpleasant condition less severe; 缓解、减轻.

**Collocations**
- alleviate pressure / pain / congestion
- alleviate concerns
- help alleviate a shortage
- measures to alleviate the problem

**Word family:** *alleviation* (noun), *alleviating* (participle).

**Example:** Clearer signs may alleviate confusion, although they will not solve every source of delay.

**Contrast:** *alleviate* means reduce severity, not necessarily eliminate a problem. Use *solve* or *eliminate* only when the evidence supports a complete remedy.

## 8. in sequence / sequentially

**Meaning:** one after another in an ordered series; 按顺序、依次.

**Collocations**
- occur in sequence
- process requests sequentially
- a sequence of steps
- follow a prescribed sequence

**Word family:** *sequence* (noun/verb), *sequential* (adjective), *sequentially* (adverb).

**Example:** The system processes urgent requests first and handles routine requests in sequence.

**Contrast:** *simultaneously* means at the same time; *sequentially* means one after another. A sequence may be fixed or flexible, so it does not automatically mean “in numerical order.”

## 9. under real-world conditions

**Meaning:** in the messy, variable circumstances in which a method is actually used; 在真实条件下、在实际环境中.

**Useful patterns**
- work under real-world conditions
- test a method under realistic conditions
- differ from results obtained in a controlled setting
- account for real-world variation

**Example:** The procedure worked well in a controlled trial, but its performance under real-world conditions remains uncertain.

**Register note:** This chunk is useful when comparing a model, simulation, laboratory test, or pilot study with routine use. It does not mean that a controlled study is worthless; it marks a difference in the conditions being discussed.
