# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-01

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-01|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-01|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary is partly connected to the National Gallery article, but the target is broader: reusable English for discussing **revision, hidden structure, physical traces, explanation, and uncertainty**.

## 1. cast something in a new light

**Meaning:** 使人从新的角度看待某件事；新的证据或背景改变了原先的理解。

### Collocations
- cast a finding in a new light
- cast the evidence in a new light
- cast an old problem in a new light
- new data cast doubt / new light on...

### Example
> The newly recovered records cast the earlier explanation in a new light.

### Contrast
This does not necessarily mean the old interpretation was completely wrong. It means the new information changes how it should be understood.

---

## 2. undergo revision

**Meaning:** 经历修改、修订或重新设计。

### Collocations
- undergo substantial revision
- undergo repeated revision
- a plan undergoes revision
- a manuscript undergoes revision
- revised version

### Example
> The protocol underwent substantial revision after the pilot study exposed several practical problems.

### Word family
- **revise** (v.)
- **revision** (n.)
- **revised** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **edit** often refers to local changes in wording, presentation, or detail;
- **revise** can involve changing structure, interpretation, or design more substantially.

---

## 3. underlying

**Meaning:** 潜在的、底层的、表面之下的；常指原因、机制、结构或假设。

### Collocations
- underlying cause
- underlying mechanism
- underlying structure
- underlying assumption
- underlying layer

### Example
> The visible symptom disappeared, but the underlying cause had not been addressed.

### Contrast
- **hidden** = simply not visible or known;
- **underlying** = usually more analytical, suggesting something fundamental beneath the surface.

---

## 4. retain

**Meaning:** 保留、保持；某个部分在变化之后仍然存在。

### Collocations
- retain its original form
- retain information
- retain control
- retain a feature
- retain much of the structure

### Example
> The redesigned interface retained the original navigation structure while simplifying the visual layout.

### Word family
- **retention** (n.)

### Contrast
- **keep** is broad and conversational;
- **retain** is more formal and often emphasizes continuity despite change.

---

## 5. trace

**Meaning:** 痕迹；也可作动词表示追踪来源、发展或因果链。

### Collocations
- a trace of
- leave traces
- detect a trace
- trace the origin of
- trace a change back to
- trace the sequence of events

### Example
> Engineers traced the failure back to a small defect introduced during assembly.

### Contrast
As a noun, **trace** usually means a small remaining sign. As a verb, it often means reconstructing a path backward through evidence.

---

## 6. reconstruct

**Meaning:** 根据不完整证据重建某个过程、事件、结构或早期状态。

### Collocations
- reconstruct an event
- reconstruct a sequence
- reconstruct the original design
- reconstruct past conditions
- digital reconstruction

### Example
> Investigators used timestamps from several systems to reconstruct the sequence of events.

### Word family
- **reconstruction** (n.)

### Contrast
- **restore** = bring an object or system back toward an earlier condition;
- **reconstruct** = infer or recreate what the earlier condition probably was.

You can reconstruct a past event even when you cannot restore it.

---

## 7. deliberate

**Meaning:** 形容词时表示“有意的、经过考虑的”；副词形式为 **deliberately**。

### Collocations
- deliberate choice
- deliberate change
- deliberate decision
- deliberate contrast
- act deliberately

### Example
> The repeated pattern suggests that the change was deliberate rather than accidental.

### Pronunciation / grammar note
As an adjective, **deliberate** describes something intentional. As a verb, **deliberate on/about** means to consider a question carefully before deciding.

### Contrast
- **deliberate** = intentional and considered;
- **accidental** = unintended;
- **spontaneous** = unplanned, though not necessarily accidental.

---

## 8. alter

**Meaning:** 改变，使某个特征、状态或结果不同。

### Collocations
- alter the appearance of
- alter a pattern
- alter the outcome
- substantially alter
- slightly alter
- remain unaltered

### Example
> A small change in sampling procedure can alter the apparent distribution of the data.

### Word family
- **alteration** (n.)

### Contrast
- **change** is the broadest term;
- **alter** is common in formal prose and often suggests modifying something that still remains recognizably the same object or system.

---

## 9. account for

**Meaning:** 解释、说明某种结果为何出现；也可表示“占据某比例”，今天重点是“解释”。

### Collocations
- account for the difference
- account for the pattern
- account for the discrepancy
- fail to account for
- cannot fully account for

### Example
> The proposed mechanism accounts for part of the pattern but does not explain why the effect disappears in one subgroup.

### Contrast
- **describe** tells us what happened;
- **account for** provides an explanation for why it happened.

A model can fit some observations without fully accounting for them.

---

## Reusable mini-patterns

> New evidence **cast the earlier interpretation in a new light**.

> The design **underwent substantial revision** after testing.

> The visible effect may reflect an **underlying mechanism**.

> The final version **retained** several features of the original.

> A faint **trace** remained after the surface was changed.

> Researchers **reconstructed** the sequence from incomplete records.

> The repeated modification appears to have been **deliberate**.

> The intervention **altered** the outcome without replacing the whole system.

> The simple explanation does not fully **account for** the observed pattern.
