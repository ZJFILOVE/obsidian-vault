# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-05

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-05|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-05|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary is partly connected to the opportunity-cost article, but the target is broader: reusable English for **choices, allocation, trade-offs, irreversible decisions, and careful comparison**.

## 1. trade-off

**Meaning:** 权衡；为了得到某种好处而接受另一种成本或损失。

### Collocations
- make a trade-off
- involve a trade-off
- trade-off between speed and accuracy
- unavoidable trade-off
- policy trade-off

### Example
> Extending battery life may involve a trade-off between performance and power consumption.

### Contrast
A **compromise** often describes an agreement between people or positions. A **trade-off** is broader: improving one dimension may worsen another even when no negotiation is involved.

---

## 2. forgo

**Meaning:** 放弃本来可以拥有、做或获得的东西。

### Collocations
- forgo income
- forgo an opportunity
- forgo a benefit
- choose to forgo
- forgo short-term gains

### Example
> Students who take an unpaid internship may forgo short-term income in exchange for experience.

### Usage note
**forgo** is the preferred spelling when you mean “go without / give up.” **Forego** historically also means “go before,” although modern usage sometimes overlaps.

---

## 3. allocate

**Meaning:** 分配资源、时间、资金或注意力。

### Collocations
- allocate resources
- allocate funding
- allocate time to
- allocate a budget
- resource allocation

### Example
> The department allocated more time to maintenance after repeated equipment failures.

### Word family
- **allocation** (n.)
- **reallocate** (v.)

### Contrast
**Distribute** emphasizes giving things out. **Allocate** emphasizes deciding how limited resources should be assigned.

---

## 4. weigh A against B

**Meaning:** 把 A 与 B 放在一起权衡，比较收益、成本、风险或证据。

### Collocations
- weigh benefits against costs
- weigh convenience against privacy
- weigh short-term gains against long-term risks
- weigh evidence against alternative explanations

### Example
> Before adopting the new system, the team weighed faster processing against the risk of more false positives.

### Contrast
**Compare A with B** is neutral. **Weigh A against B** implies a decision or judgment.

---

## 5. implicit cost

**Meaning:** 隐性成本；没有直接以账单或付款形式出现，但因为选择某项行动而失去的价值。

### Collocations
- implicit cost of time
- account for implicit costs
- explicit and implicit costs
- overlook an implicit cost

### Example
> The course fee was low, but the weekly travel time created a substantial implicit cost.

### Contrast
An **explicit cost** is directly paid or recorded. An **implicit cost** often involves time, lost alternatives, or resources already owned.

---

## 6. sunk cost

**Meaning:** 沉没成本；已经发生且无法收回的成本。

### Collocations
- sunk-cost fallacy
- treat X as a sunk cost
- ignore sunk costs in a forward-looking decision
- recover a sunk cost

### Example
> The money already spent on the failed prototype is a sunk cost and should not by itself determine whether the project continues.

### Contrast: sunk cost vs. opportunity cost
- **sunk cost:** already incurred; backward-looking
- **opportunity cost:** value of the best alternative forgone; forward-looking

Do not confuse “I already spent it” with “I would give this up if I choose option A.”

---

## 7. marginal

**Meaning:** 边际的；由“再多一个单位”带来的额外变化。也可表示很小、有限。

### Collocations
- marginal benefit
- marginal cost
- marginal improvement
- marginal increase
- marginal effect

### Example
> Adding a tenth sensor produced only a marginal improvement because the main coverage gaps were elsewhere.

### Contrast
**Average** asks what happens across all units. **Marginal** asks what changes when one additional unit is added or one more step is taken.

---

## 8. lock in

**Meaning:** 锁定；使某个选择、价格、技术路径或约束难以改变。

### Collocations
- lock in a price
- lock in a decision
- lock in long-term costs
- become locked into a system
- technological lock-in

### Example
> A cheap platform can become expensive later if it locks the organization into a proprietary format.

### Contrast
**Commit to** emphasizes a decision or promise. **Lock in** emphasizes reduced flexibility after the decision.

---

## 9. be better off

**Meaning:** 处境更好；在某种比较标准下获得更好的总体结果。

### Collocations
- be financially better off
- be better off doing X
- leave someone better off
- no better off than before

### Example
> A commuter may be better off taking a slower train if the journey is cheaper and the travel time can be used productively.

### Precision note
“Better off” always depends on the criterion: money, time, stress, flexibility, health, or something else. State the dimension when it matters.

## Reusable mini-patterns

> Every policy involves some **trade-offs**.

> Choosing one option means **forgoing** another.

> We need to **allocate limited resources** across competing priorities.

> The committee **weighed the expected benefits against the risks**.

> Travel time is an **implicit cost** even when the ticket is cheap.

> Money already spent may be a **sunk cost**.

> The latest change produced only a **marginal improvement**.

> A long contract can **lock in** future costs.

> Users may still **be better off** if the higher price buys reliability.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise item.

1. A resource has already been spent and cannot be recovered: **sunk cost / opportunity cost**.
2. You choose research training instead of a paid summer job: the salary you give up is part of the **opportunity cost / sunk cost**.
3. A one-percent improvement after a major redesign is **marginal / total**.
4. A contract makes it difficult to switch suppliers for five years: it **locks in / merely compares** the organization.
5. You compare convenience with privacy before deciding: **weigh A against B / distribute A to B**.
6. You decide how much staff time each project receives: **allocate / imitate**.
7. You voluntarily give up a short-term bonus: **forgo / recover**.
8. An unpaid two-hour commute creates a time burden even though no fee is charged: **implicit cost / invoice**.
9. A person gains more free time even though they earn slightly less: they may still **be better off / be identical** depending on their priorities.

> 2. **opportunity cost**
> 3. **marginal**
> 4. **locks in**
> 5. **weigh A against B**
> 6. **allocate**
> 7. **forgo**
> 8. **implicit cost**
> 9. **be better off**
