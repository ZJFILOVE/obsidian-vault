# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-14

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-14|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-14|Today's translation]]

Today’s focus is **reusable language for describing historical change, systems, and consequences**. Some items connect to the coffee article, but all are useful in broader academic or journalistic English.

## 1. commodity

**Meaning:** 商品；尤其是可以交易、规模化生产或具有市场价值的原料/产品

### Collocations
- a global commodity
- commodity prices
- commodity trade
- agricultural commodities

### Contrast
- **product** is very broad and can refer to almost anything sold.
- **commodity** often emphasizes exchange, markets, and large-scale trade.

### Example
> Coffee became a global commodity long before modern café culture emerged.

## 2. fuel (verb)

**Meaning:** 推动；加剧；为……提供动力

### Collocations
- fuel economic growth
- fuel demand
- fuel conflict
- fuel speculation
- fuel public debate

### Usage note
As a verb, **fuel** does not mean that something is the only cause. It often means that it **adds energy to an existing process**.

### Example
> Cheap credit can fuel demand without being the sole cause of rising prices.

## 3. be tied to

**Meaning:** 与……密切相关；与……相联系

### Collocations
- be closely tied to trade
- be tied to political change
- be tied to local conditions

### Contrast
- **be related to** is neutral and broad.
- **be tied to** often suggests a stronger structural or historical connection.

### Example
> The expansion of a crop may be tied to labor systems, transport networks, and consumer demand.

## 4. facilitate

**Meaning:** 促进；使……更容易发生

### Collocations
- facilitate communication
- facilitate trade
- facilitate access to information
- facilitate cooperation

### Word family
- facilitate (v.)
- facilitation (n.)
- facilitator (n.)

### Example
> Better transport infrastructure can facilitate trade between previously isolated regions.

## 5. widespread

**Meaning:** 广泛的；普遍存在的

### Collocations
- widespread use
- widespread adoption
- widespread concern
- widespread inequality

### Contrast
- **popular** usually means liked or commonly chosen.
- **widespread** means present across many places, people, or contexts, even if it is not liked.

### Example
> A technology can become widespread before its long-term effects are fully understood.

## 6. reinforce

**Meaning:** 加强；巩固；使已有趋势、结构或观念更强

### Collocations
- reinforce a trend
- reinforce inequality
- reinforce a belief
- mutually reinforcing factors

### Word family
- reinforce (v.)
- reinforcement (n.)

### Example
> Institutions can reinforce habits that originally emerged for very different reasons.

## 7. cultivate

**Meaning:** 耕种；培养；逐步发展

### Collocations
- cultivate coffee / crops
- cultivate a skill
- cultivate trust
- cultivate a habit

### Useful semantic link
The agricultural sense and the abstract sense are closely connected: just as crops are deliberately grown over time, skills, habits, and relationships can be **cultivated** gradually.

### Example
> Regular discussion can help cultivate the habit of questioning weak evidence.

## 8. intervention

**Meaning:** 干预；介入；介入措施

### Collocations
- government intervention
- policy intervention
- military intervention
- an effective intervention

### Word family
- intervene (v.)
- intervention (n.)

### Example
> A policy intervention may solve one problem while creating new incentives elsewhere.

## 9. unintended consequence

**Meaning:** 非预期后果

### Collocations
- produce unintended consequences
- avoid unintended consequences
- an unintended consequence of a policy

### Example
> A rule designed to reduce costs may have the unintended consequence of lowering service quality.

## Useful contrasts

### cause vs. contribute to vs. facilitate vs. reinforce

- **cause** — 直接表述因果，语气最强。
- **contribute to** — 是若干原因之一。
- **facilitate** — 使某件事更容易发生。
- **reinforce** — 强化已经存在的趋势或结构。

Compare:

> New technology **caused** the change.  
> New technology **contributed to** the change.  
> New technology **facilitated** the change.  
> New technology **reinforced** an existing trend.

These sentences make different causal claims. Choosing the right verb is part of precise academic writing.
