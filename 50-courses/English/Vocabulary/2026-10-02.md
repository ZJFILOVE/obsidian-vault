# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-02

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-02|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-02|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary partly connects to the shopping-cart article, but the target is broader: reusable English for discussing **launches, user adoption, resistance, design refinement, and practical fit**.

## 1. rollout

**Meaning:** 推出、部署、上线的过程，尤其用于产品、服务、政策或系统。

### Collocations
- initial rollout
- phased rollout
- nationwide rollout
- rollout plan
- during the rollout

### Example
> The initial rollout reached only a small group of users so that the team could identify operational problems before expanding the service.

### Contrast
- **launch** often emphasizes the moment something is introduced;
- **rollout** emphasizes the process of introducing it across users, locations, or stages.

A product can be launched on one day but rolled out over several months.

---

## 2. uptake

**Meaning:** 采用率、接受程度；某个产品、服务、政策或行为真正被用户采用的程度。

### Collocations
- user uptake
- low / high uptake
- increase uptake
- encourage uptake
- uptake remains limited

### Example
> The service was technically available across campus, but uptake remained low because many students did not know it existed.

### Contrast
- **availability** = something can be used;
- **uptake** = people actually begin using it.

Availability does not guarantee uptake.

---

## 3. gain traction

**Meaning:** 逐渐获得支持、使用者或实际进展；“开始真正推进起来”。

### Collocations
- begin to gain traction
- gain traction among users
- gain political / commercial / academic traction
- struggle to gain traction

### Example
> The new booking system gained traction only after the mobile interface was simplified.

### Contrast
**Go viral** means spreading extremely quickly, often online. **Gain traction** is broader and usually slower: an idea, project, technology, or proposal starts to attract real support or use.

---

## 4. be met with resistance

**Meaning:** 遭到抵制、反对或不愿接受。

### Collocations
- be met with strong resistance
- encounter resistance
- resistance to change
- user resistance
- overcome resistance

### Example
> The new workflow was met with resistance because staff believed it added extra steps without solving the underlying problem.

### Contrast
- **disagreement** = people think differently;
- **resistance** = people actively or behaviorally resist a change.

Resistance may be emotional, practical, institutional, or economic.

---

## 5. normalize a practice

**Meaning:** 使某种做法逐渐成为“正常、常见、无需特别解释”的行为。

### Collocations
- normalize a behavior
- normalize remote work
- normalize a practice
- become normalized
- social normalization

### Example
> Visible use by respected peers can help normalize a new practice even before formal rules change.

### Contrast
- **standardize** = make procedures or specifications consistent;
- **normalize** = make a behavior or idea socially ordinary or accepted.

A practice can be normalized without being technically standardized.

---

## 6. precursor

**Meaning:** 前身、先驱；更早出现并预示或影响后续形式的事物。

### Collocations
- a precursor to
- early precursor
- direct precursor
- technological precursor
- serve as a precursor to

### Example
> Early electronic organizers were important precursors to many functions now built into smartphones.

### Contrast
- **predecessor** often means the previous version or holder in a direct sequence;
- **precursor** can be looser: an earlier development that anticipates or contributes to what comes later.

---

## 7. refine

**Meaning:** 改进、完善，使设计、方法、解释或表达更精确、更有效。

### Collocations
- refine a design
- refine a method
- refine an estimate
- refine the model
- continuously refine

### Example
> Pilot testing helped the team refine the interface before the full rollout.

### Word family
- **refinement** (n.)
- **refined** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **revise** can involve substantial restructuring;
- **refine** usually suggests improving something that already works at least partly.

---

## 8. hinge on

**Meaning:** 取决于；强调某个结果关键地依赖某个条件。

### Collocations
- success hinges on
- the decision hinges on
- whether X works hinges on
- hinge largely on

### Example
> Whether the program scales successfully may hinge on maintenance rather than on the number of devices purchased.

### Contrast
**Depend on** is the general expression. **Hinge on** is stronger and suggests a particularly decisive condition.

---

## 9. accommodate

**Meaning:** 容纳；适应；为某类人、需求或约束留出空间。

### Collocations
- accommodate more users
- accommodate different needs
- accommodate demand
- accommodate a larger volume
- be designed to accommodate

### Example
> The redesigned waiting area can accommodate more passengers without blocking the main entrance.

### Word family
- **accommodation** (n.)
- **accommodating** (adj., context-dependent)

### Contrast
- **adapt to** = the system or person changes in response to conditions;
- **accommodate** = make room for, support, or handle those conditions.

---

## Reusable mini-patterns

> The **initial rollout** revealed several practical problems.

> The service was available, but **uptake remained limited**.

> The idea began to **gain traction** after users saw clear benefits.

> The proposal was **met with resistance** from people who disliked the extra steps.

> Repeated exposure can **normalize a practice**.

> An earlier device served as a **precursor to** the modern design.

> The team **refined the interface** after observing real users.

> Long-term success may **hinge on** maintenance and trust.

> The system must **accommodate** both peak demand and ordinary use.

## Contrast drill

1. The service was technically available, but few people used it: **low uptake / low existence**.
2. The product was introduced gradually across ten cities: **rollout / memory**.
3. The idea began attracting real users after several months: **gained traction / exploded chemically**.
4. Staff actively pushed back against the new workflow: it was **met with resistance / merely unknown**.

6. An early handheld organizer came before the smartphone and anticipated some of its functions: **precursor / replacement**.
7. Testing produced small but useful improvements: the team **refined / destroyed** the design.
8. The outcome depends critically on battery life: success **hinges on / decorates** battery performance.
