# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-09-29

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-09-29|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-09-29|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary is partly connected to source criticism, but every item is reusable in research, journalism, science, presentations, and argumentative writing. The goal is not to memorize eight Chinese equivalents; learn the **collocations and evidential function** of each item.

## 1. firsthand account

**Meaning:** 第一手叙述；由亲历者或直接见证者提供的描述。

### Collocations
- a firsthand account of...
- provide a firsthand account
- rely on firsthand accounts
- compare several firsthand accounts
- firsthand testimony / firsthand evidence

### Example
> A firsthand account can reveal details that later summaries omit, but it may still contain memory errors or selective reporting.

### Contrast
- **firsthand account** = 亲历者的叙述
- **secondary account** = 后来根据其他材料写成的叙述
- **eyewitness account** = 特指目击某一事件的叙述

A firsthand account is **not automatically more accurate**; it is closer to the event, not magically free of error.

## 2. corroborate

**Meaning:** 用独立证据证实、加强某个说法的可信度。常见于法律、历史、新闻和科研。

### Collocations
- corroborate a claim
- corroborate an account
- corroborate the evidence
- be corroborated by independent records
- seek corroborating evidence

### Example
> The research team checked maintenance logs to corroborate the participants' recollections.

### Word family
- **corroboration** (n.)
- **corroborating** (adj.)

### Contrast
- **confirm** = 很宽泛，可以表示确认信息、预约、结果
- **corroborate** = 强调另一条证据与原有说法相互支持

A useful question is: **Is the second source genuinely independent, or is it copying the first?**

## 3. contextualize

**Meaning:** 把信息放回它产生的背景中理解，而不是孤立解读。

### Collocations
- contextualize a statement
- contextualize the data
- place a source in context
- historical / social context
- contextual information

### Example
> A salary figure is difficult to interpret unless it is contextualized by local prices, working hours, and the year in which it was reported.

### Word family
- **context** (n.)
- **contextual** (adj.)
- **contextualization** (n.)

### Contrast
- **explain** = 一般性的解释
- **contextualize** = 特别强调背景会改变信息的意义

## 4. provenance

**Meaning:** 来源与流传记录；某件材料从哪里来、由谁产生、后来如何保存或转移。

### Collocations
- establish provenance
- document provenance
- uncertain provenance
- provenance record
- data provenance

### Example
> Before using the dataset, the researchers checked its provenance to understand how the records had been collected and modified.

### Use beyond museums
Although **provenance** is common in art and archives, it is also used in **data management, scientific workflows, digital forensics, and AI datasets**.

### Etymology note
The useful connection is with French *provenir*, “to come from.” The practical memory hook is therefore **where something came from**, not a folk story about the word.

## 5. weigh the evidence

**Meaning:** 权衡证据；综合考虑证据的强弱、来源、相互关系和局限。

### Collocations
- carefully weigh the evidence
- weigh competing evidence
- weigh the evidence for and against...
- after weighing the evidence
- evidence carries more / less weight

### Example
> After weighing the evidence from several independent measurements, the team concluded that the anomaly was probably real.

### Contrast
- **collect evidence** = 收集证据
- **weigh evidence** = 判断不同证据应当占多大分量

This chunk is especially useful when sources conflict.

## 6. tentative conclusion

**Meaning:** 暂时性结论；目前证据支持，但仍可能随着新证据而修改。

### Collocations
- reach a tentative conclusion
- draw a tentative conclusion
- a tentative explanation
- remain tentative
- treat a finding as tentative

### Example
> With only one month of data, the safest interpretation is a tentative conclusion rather than a firm trend claim.

### Contrast
- **tentative** = 暂定、谨慎、尚未最终确认
- **uncertain** = 不确定，范围更广
- **speculative** = 推测性更强，证据通常更弱

A tentative conclusion can still be well reasoned; it simply acknowledges limited evidence.

## 7. take something at face value

**Meaning:** 不加核查地按表面意思接受某个说法、数字或材料。

### Collocations
- take a claim at face value
- should not be taken at face value
- accept a figure at face value
- rather than taking X at face value

### Example
> A dramatic percentage should not be taken at face value until you know the sample size and the baseline used for comparison.

### Contrast
- **believe** = 相信
- **take at face value** = 特别强调没有进一步核查或追问条件

This is highly reusable in media literacy and academic discussion.

## 8. be subject to

**Meaning:** 可能受到……影响；受……约束；需遵守……。这是一个高频正式结构，具体意思由后面的名词决定。

### Collocations
- be subject to error
- be subject to bias
- be subject to change
- be subject to review
- be subject to approval
- be subject to limitations

### Example
> Self-reported data are subject to recall error, especially when participants are asked about events from years earlier.

### Contrast
- **be subjected to** = “被迫经历 / 被施加”，例如 *be subjected to extreme heat*
- **be subject to** = “容易受到 / 受制于 / 需遵守”

Do not add **-ed** unless you really mean that something is being actively imposed on the subject.

## Reusable mini-patterns

### Source type
> A **firsthand account** can provide unique detail without being automatically reliable.

### Independent support
> The claim becomes stronger when it is **corroborated by** an independent source.

### Background
> The number should be **contextualized** before it is compared across years.

### Origin
> Clear **provenance** helps readers understand where the data came from and how they were processed.

### Conflicting information
> We should **weigh the evidence** rather than simply count how many sources agree.

### Uncertainty
> The available evidence supports only a **tentative conclusion**.

### Verification
> A striking claim should not be **taken at face value**.

### Limitations
> Any retrospective account is **subject to** some degree of memory error.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise option.

1. The journalist looked for an independent record to **corroborate / decorate** the witness's account.
2. A document should be **contextualized / isolated more strongly** before its meaning is generalized.
3. Researchers recorded the **provenance / popularity** of each dataset so later users could trace its origin.
4. Historians must **weigh the evidence / count the evidence only** when sources disagree.
5. With such a small sample, the finding should remain **tentative / permanent**.
6. A corporate press release should not automatically be **taken at face value / taken to the face**.
7. Interview data collected years later may be **subject to / subjected by** recall error.
8. A **firsthand account / secondhand device** may contain details unavailable in later summaries.

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. **corroborate** — the second record provides independent support.
> 2. **contextualized** — background is needed to interpret meaning.
> 3. **provenance** — the issue is origin and processing history.
> 4. **weigh the evidence** — quality and independence matter, not only quantity.
> 5. **tentative** — the conclusion should remain provisional.
> 6. **taken at face value** — accepted without sufficient verification.
> 7. **subject to** — vulnerable to or affected by recall error.
> 8. **firsthand account** — a direct participant or witness describes the event.

## Retrieval quiz

Try without looking back.

1. What is the difference between a **firsthand account** and an **eyewitness account**?
2. Complete: Independent maintenance records ______ the worker's account of when the machine failed.
3. Which verb means “place information in the background needed to interpret it properly”?
4. What does **provenance** tell you about a document or dataset?
5. Complete: When sources conflict, researchers should ______ the evidence rather than simply count sources.
6. Why might a careful researcher call a conclusion **tentative**?
7. Complete: A large percentage should not be taken ______ ______ until the baseline is known.
8. Choose: Survey responses are **subject to / subjected to** recall error.
9. Why do ten websites repeating the same press release not necessarily provide ten independent pieces of corroboration?
10. Rewrite more carefully: *It is a primary source, so it proves what happened.*

> [!answer]- Answers
> 1. A firsthand account comes from someone directly involved in or experiencing the situation; an eyewitness account specifically comes from someone who directly saw the event in question.
> 2. corroborated
> 3. contextualize
> 4. Its origin, chain of custody or transmission, and often how it was created or processed.
> 5. weigh
> 6. Because the current evidence supports an interpretation but is still limited enough that new evidence could reasonably change it.
> 7. at face value
> 8. **subject to**
> 9. They may all depend on the same original source, so repetition does not create independent evidence.
> 10. A primary source provides direct evidence from a participant, witness, or contemporaneous record, but its claims still need to be contextualized and, where possible, corroborated.

## Production drill

Write one original sentence for each item:

1. **firsthand account** — laboratory, accident, or campus event
2. **corroborate** — two independent records
3. **contextualize** — a number or quotation
4. **provenance** — dataset, image, or artifact
5. **weigh the evidence** — conflicting sources
6. **tentative conclusion** — incomplete data
7. **take at face value** — headline or press release
8. **be subject to** — error, change, review, or limitations

Then combine your best four sentences into one coherent paragraph about how to evaluate a claim.
