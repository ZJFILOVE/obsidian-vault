# Vocabulary Practice — 2026-10-06

[[50-courses/English/English|English]] · [[50-courses/English/Daily/2026-10-06|Today's input]] · [[50-courses/English/Translation/2026-10-06|Today's translation]]

Today's vocabulary is partly connected to the Smithsonian article, but the aim is broader: reusable English for discussing **community initiatives, access, participation, intergenerational transfer, and whether a practice can remain viable over time**.

## 1. grassroots

**Meaning:** 基层自发的；由普通成员或当地社区推动，而不是主要由大型机构自上而下推动。

### Collocations
- grassroots initiative
- grassroots organization
- grassroots campaign
- grassroots support
- grassroots network

### Example
> The recycling program began as a grassroots initiative run by residents before the city provided formal support.

### Contrast
**Grassroots** describes where an initiative is organized or supported from. It does not automatically mean the initiative is informal, small, or effective.

---

## 2. be designed for

**Meaning:** 为某一用户、目的或使用场景而设计。

### Collocations
- designed for beginners
- designed for everyday use
- designed for local conditions
- specifically designed for
- be designed with users in mind

### Example
> A navigation app designed for drivers may work poorly for cyclists if route assumptions are different.

### Contrast
**Designed for** focuses on intended users or purposes. **Used by** describes actual users. The two do not always match.

---

## 3. bridge a gap

**Meaning:** 缩小、弥合两个群体、资源、能力或状态之间的差距。

### Collocations
- bridge the gap between A and B
- bridge a knowledge gap
- bridge the digital divide
- help bridge a gap
- bridge gaps in access

### Example
> Evening office hours can help bridge the gap between students who have flexible schedules and those who work during the day.

### Contrast
**Fill a gap** often means supply something missing. **Bridge a gap** emphasizes connecting two sides that are separated.

---

## 4. on one's own terms

**Meaning:** 按照自己的条件、方式或价值判断来做某事，而不是完全由别人规定。

### Collocations
- participate on one's own terms
- define success on one's own terms
- engage with technology on one's own terms
- tell a story on one's own terms

### Example
> The archive allows families to describe their own photographs on their own terms rather than relying only on labels written by outsiders.

### Usage note
This phrase often carries the idea of agency or control. It is useful in discussions of education, work, culture, negotiation, and personal decisions.

---

## 5. gain access to

**Meaning:** 获得进入、使用或接触某种资源、服务、知识或机会的渠道。

### Collocations
- gain access to education
- gain access to data
- gain access to services
- gain access to funding
- gain access to information

### Example
> Remote students gained access to laboratory demonstrations through a new video platform.

### Contrast
**Have access to** describes a state. **Gain access to** emphasizes the change from not having access to having it.

---

## 6. pass down

**Meaning:** 把知识、传统、物品、故事或习惯从上一代传给下一代。

### Collocations
- pass down a tradition
- pass down knowledge
- pass down stories
- be passed down through generations
- pass something down to children

### Example
> Many practical skills are passed down through repeated participation rather than through formal instruction.

### Contrast
**Transmit** is more formal and technical. **Pass down** is common when the transfer is intergenerational.

---

## 7. be confined to

**Meaning:** 被限制在某个范围、场景、地点或群体之内。

### Collocations
- be confined to the classroom
- be confined to a small region
- be confined to specialists
- not be confined to
- remain confined to

### Example
> If a skill is practiced only during exams, its use may remain confined to the classroom.

### Precision note
**Confined to** suggests a boundary or limitation. It can describe physical space, but it is also widely used for ideas, effects, and practices.

---

## 8. underrepresented

**Meaning:** 代表不足的；某群体在某个领域、样本、机构或数据集中所占比例明显偏低。

### Collocations
- underrepresented group
- underrepresented in research
- underrepresented in the dataset
- historically underrepresented
- remain underrepresented

### Example
> Rural users were underrepresented in the pilot study, so the results may not fully describe their needs.

### Contrast
**Minority** refers to relative size in a population. **Underrepresented** refers to insufficient representation in a particular setting. A numerically large group can still be underrepresented in a dataset.

---

## 9. sustain

**Meaning:** 维持、支撑，使某种活动、效果、系统或状态能够持续。

### Collocations
- sustain long-term growth
- sustain participation
- sustain a program
- sustain interest
- financially sustainable
- environmental sustainability

### Example
> Initial funding can launch a project, but stable staffing may be necessary to sustain it.

### Word family
- **sustainable** (adj.)
- **sustainability** (n.)
- **sustained** (adj.)

### Contrast
**Maintain** often means keep something at a current level or condition. **Sustain** often emphasizes keeping it going over time despite demands or pressures.

## Reusable mini-patterns

> The project began as a **grassroots initiative**.

> The system was **designed for** users with limited bandwidth.

> The program aims to **bridge the gap between** availability and actual use.

> Communities should be able to participate **on their own terms**.

> The change helped more people **gain access to** the service.

> Skills can be **passed down through generations**.

> The benefits should not be **confined to** a small group.

> Some users were **underrepresented** in the original study.

> Short-term enthusiasm is easier to create than to **sustain**.

## Contrast drill

Choose the more precise expression.

1. A project is organized mainly by local residents: **grassroots / top-down**.
2. A tool was created specifically for older adults: **designed for / accidentally seen by** older adults.
3. A new program connects students who lack equipment with a lending service: it helps **bridge a gap / widen a gap**.
4. A community chooses how its own materials are described: it works **on its own terms / without any agency**.
5. Students who previously could not use the database can now log in: they **gained access to / lost access to** it.
6. A craft is taught from grandparents to children: it is **passed down / phased out**.
7. A benefit appears only in one department: it is **confined to / distributed across** the whole university.
8. A group forms 30% of users but only 5% of the study sample: it is **underrepresented / overrepresented**.
9. A project receives enough staffing to continue for years: the staffing helps **sustain / interrupt** it.

> 4. **on its own terms**
> 5. **gained access to**
> 6. **passed down**
> 7. **confined to**
> 8. **underrepresented**
