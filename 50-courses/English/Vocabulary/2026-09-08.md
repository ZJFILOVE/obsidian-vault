## 1. encode
**v. 编码；把信息转换成可储存或处理的形式**

### Useful combinations
- encode information
- encode a memory
- encoding process

### Word-building memory
**en-** can mean “put into / cause to be in” + **code**. Think: turn an experience **into a code** the system can use.

### Example
> Paying close attention helps the brain encode new information more effectively.

**Word family:** encode → encoding → decoder / decode

---

## 2. retrieve
**v. 找回；检索；重新取得**

### Collocations
- retrieve information
- retrieve a memory
- data retrieval

### Memory note
The word came through French from a root associated with “finding again.” For learning, connect it directly with **retrieval practice**: trying to pull knowledge out of memory rather than putting the same page in front of your eyes again.

### Example
> Testing yourself forces you to retrieve information without looking at your notes.

**Word family:** retrieve → retrieval → retrievable

---

## 3. consolidate
**v. 巩固；合并；使稳固**

### Root
Latin **con-** (together) + **solidus** (solid) → “make solid together.” This gives a useful mental picture for **memory consolidation**: a new memory becomes more stable.

### Collocations
- consolidate a memory
- consolidate knowledge
- consolidate gains

### Example
> Sleep appears to play an important role in consolidating newly learned information.

**Word family:** consolidate → consolidation → consolidated

---

## 4. vivid
**adj. 生动的；鲜明的；清晰的**

### Root
Related to Latin **vivus** = alive. The same broad root family appears in **vital**, **revive**, and **survive**. A **vivid memory** feels almost “alive.”

### Collocations
- a vivid memory
- a vivid description
- vivid detail

### Example
> She still has a vivid memory of her first day at university.

---

## 5. association
**n. 联想；联系；协会**

### Collocations
- form an association
- association between A and B
- word association

### Word family
associate (v./n.) → associated (adj.) → association (n.)

### Example
> Creating an unusual association can make a new word easier to remember.

---

## 6. reinforce
**v. 加强；强化；巩固**

### Word-building memory
**re-** (again) + **inforce/enforce**, historically related to making something stronger. In modern English, think simply: **add strength again**.

### Collocations
- reinforce a habit
- reinforce learning
- reinforce the idea that...

### Example
> Reviewing a concept after a delay can reinforce the original learning.

**Word family:** reinforce → reinforcement

---

## 7. retain
**v. 保留；保持；记住**

### Root
Latin **re-** (back) + **tenere** (hold) → “hold back / keep.” The **-tain** family is extremely useful: **retain, maintain, contain, obtain**.

### Collocations
- retain information
- retain control
- retain employees

### Example
> Students retain more when they revisit material over several days.

**Word family:** retain → retention

---

## 8. readily available
**chunk. 随手可得的；容易获得的**

This is more natural in formal English than repeatedly using *very easy to get*.

### Collocations
- information is readily available
- readily available evidence
- make resources readily available

### Example
> Because information is readily available online, remembering where to verify it has become increasingly important.

---

## 9. mistake A for B
**chunk. 把 A 误认为 B**

### Pattern
> mistake familiarity **for** understanding

Do not use *mistake A as B* in this meaning.

### Example
> Learners sometimes mistake recognition for genuine mastery.
